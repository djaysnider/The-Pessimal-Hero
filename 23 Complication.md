Vicky was exhausted when she finally pulled into the parking lot of her apartment complex.  She parked diagonally, because that’s what Justice looked like at 2 AM. She was thrilled to be productive and helping, but the late nights were starting to wear on her.  She wasn’t twenty-five anymore.  She couldn’t even pretend to be twenty-five.  Fortunately, tomorrow was Sunday, and with Hal nursing a sprained ankle, she was happy to sleep in for once.  She told the boys she’d call after lunch and maybe meet up with them to go over some strategy, but she’d make that decision in the morning after intense negotiations with her pillow.
Despite her better judgment, or maybe because of it, she really had come to think of Barry and Hal as friends, not just kids she was mothering through a difficult situation she understood all too well.  She was pretty sure she hadn’t lied when she said Barry was feeling useful for the first time in a long time, but she realized she may have been speaking more about herself.
“Good evening, Miss Montanna,” a voice said from the shadows near the door to her apartment.
Instinctively, Vicky’s hand went for the taser in her purse, only it wasn’t there.  She’d given it to Hal to play superhero with, and he’d failed spectacularly the one time he had a chance to put it to good use.  She was disappointed at that more than anything. Completely unarmed and without a viable retreat, she froze, ready to beat the living tar out of whomever walked out of that shadow like a mother whose discovered her kid spent the rent money on baseball cards. 
A man in a long tan coat and crumpled brown suit emerged from the shadows under the stairs to the second-floor apartments.  She let out a sigh of relief and pulled her hand out of her purse.
“Sammy,” she said calmly.  “To what do I owe the late-night jump scare?”
“I’m sure you know.”
“Indulge me, Boo-Boo.  Besides, I know you’ve been working on this speech for the past two hours.”
“We keep you on a loose leash, but it doesn’t mean we don’t watch you, Vicky.  Tapping into the metropolitan police tactical computers, pulling urban warfare cookbooks off the darkweb, and tonight placing a well-disguised and well-informed 911 call from a public hotspot in New Holland about a robbery in progress on the northwest side of the city.”
“You’ll find I didn’t tamper with anything in the Police systems, and I’ll send them a complete report on their security flaws, along with my consulting bill.” 
“You realize I could haul you back in--”
“---You threaten to haul me back in every time I see you, Boo-Boo. And yet...” She gestured to herself. “Still Funkin’ for Jamaica.”
“I’ll be forced to  this time, if you don’t cooperate.”
“Well, THAT depends on what the ask is.  So far, you’ve just monologued at me like a Bond villain with bad fashion sense.”
“I’ll assume Barry Chase is also helping you assist this Eye-do-lawn character,” he continued.  “Who is he?  Some kind of mercenary?  Former special ops?  What’s his game?  What’s he after?”
“Barry who?” She didn’t even try to make it convincing.
The man smirked.  “Fine.  If you’re going to tell me you’re not involved; that you don’t know the guy; that you don’t know anything about a highly dangerous, highly trained, highly equipped urban warfare specialist declaring war on criminals in the city, then I won’t have to protect you from anything when I shut the whole operation down.”
Vicky folded her arms. “Now, why would you want to go and do a dumb thing like shutting him down? Is he getting in the way of your plans to slightly inconvenience criminals? The city needs him, Detective Clipboard.”
“You know, we can’t have amateurs out there pretending they’re Captain America.  Not only is an untrained operative a danger to innocent civilians and bystanders, but it makes the police as well as us look bad.”
“You don’t need his help for that, Boo-Boo.” She resisted the urge to use her newfound expertise to argue Sammy’s poor choice of metaphor or explain that ‘Captain America’ was currently nursing a weak ankle after slipping on an exploded Twinkie. 
“That’s exactly the problem.  We’re trying to carry out a systematic program to adjust the public’s thinking about federal and local law enforcement.  It’s at a very difficult phase right now, and one rogue vigilante like your friend there cracking skulls and saving cats can destroy months of delicate, psychological work.  Quite frankly, we may not be able to recover from the fallout if it fails.”
“Uh-huh.  Good story, Boo-Boo.  Now tell me the real reason and THEN I’ll decide if I want to help you or not.”
The man stepped back into the shadows, pulled a pack of cigarettes from his inside pocket and lit one.  His voice lowered in pitch and took on a raspy, whispery tone. “The scooter gangs,” he said, taking a long drag off his cigarette.
“Those upstart little punks?!” Oh, Sammy, that’s a bold one even for you if you think I’m gonna buy that.
He motioned for her to keep her voice down.  Collecting himself, he continued, “We’ve had a man inside for a few weeks now. It’s not at all what it seems.  There’s a new player.  He’s organized them.  He’s financing them.  They’re increasing their numbers.  Incidents involving the gangs are becoming more and more prominent, bolder and bolder.  Now there’s word he’s convincing them to settle their differences and unite.  You realize how dangerous that could be?”
Vicky thought for a moment, “Not half as scary as a hostile invasion of wine-slugging soccer moms on rollerblades. Nope, still not convinced.  Good story.  Crazy enough for me to believe, but no.”
“There’s more, a series of orders made to a shady exporter in South China.  Hundreds of thousands of dollars in equipment ordered and in the process of being shipped to front companies all over the city.  We think he’s getting ready to arm them.  This isn’t just kids mugging people and smashing windows anymore. It’s insurgency on wheels.”
“It’s a bad Fast and Furious sequel,” Vicky said.  Whether she believed him or not, the prospect was more than a little disturbing.  “Okay then, if you’re right, and all that IS true, then you need our help and you shouldn’t be wasting resources trying to shut us down.”
“I’m not wasting resources, Montana.  I could have you shut down tomorrow morning and your whole PAW Patrol in jail before brunch.  I’m asking you to do it as a courtesy.”
“Threatening me while pretending not to is peak Boo-Boo.  Either way, you think I have any say with a bad-assed vigilante like the Eidolon?”
“Well,” the man seemed to stammer for the first time, “I was ... hoping--”
“Let me tell you something, Special Agent Buzzkill,” Vicky said, taking two steps toward him.  “I didn’t start any of this.  Their team found me. All your preparations, your contingencies, and all your super clandestine secret squirrel stuff, and they saw right through it.  They found me, and they recruited me.  They’re wired in, Sammy.  They know all about you, they know all your moves, they even know where you hide the good donuts.  They’re not afraid of you, but you know what?  They’re on your side.  I’ve always worked for the good guys.  You know that, and that’s why you didn’t arrest me the minute you found out about Operation: Eidolon.”  She knew she was going off the rails, but the growing look of terror in the man’s eyes was so precious, she couldn’t resist egging him on.  “So, if this bruises your ego so bad you’ve gotta be “The Man from Overcompensate” and shut down what could be your biggest asset, then you’re gonna have to go ask THEM about it, but right now I’d be real surprised if they weren’t already expecting a visit from you and are five moves ahead.”  With that, she pulled her key out of her pocket, beat a hasty retreat into her apartment, and slammed the door behind her.  “Next time you want to spy on me in the middle of the night, bring coffee!” She yelled back to Sammy.
