Twenty minutes later, Barry pulled the van behind a strip mall just around the corner from Chessman Park, where, based on its proximity to both the Cavalier Street Library and St. Leonardo Church, Hal assumed would be the Hierophant’s staging area for launching the last phase of his grand scheme.
Hal was slipping the last armor plate into the pouch on his forearm when Barry put a hand on his shoulder. Its meaning was instantly clear to Hal.  “Not you too,” he said, disappointed.
“One way or another, I think it has to end here tonight,” Barry said fatefully.
“Barr, listen, you can’t just---”
“No, you listen, Hal.  Now I agreed to this out of a lifetime of friendship to you that I wanted to honor, and I stood by you through an awful lot, but you have to understand, one way or another, there’s only one way out of this for you, there always has been.  We never really did work on my exit strategy, and that’s okay.  That’s on me.”  He removed his hand and busied himself checking Hal’s suit and myriad weapons pouches.  “I just don’t believe in what you’re doing here.  I don’t think our moral obligations are so absolute.  Maybe there was a point where I would have gone to jail for you or even died for you and this mission, but I really don’t think you’re being the hero here and now.  You’re all caught up in your ego and need for the Eidolon to bring down the Hierophant on your own.  It’s not what we talked about. It’s not the mission.  The mission was to be a hero for saving people. Not being a hero for the sake of being a hero.”
“You don’t get it, do you?”  Hal pleaded. “The point was to give meaning to my life at the end. I’ve spent years being the sensible, quiet one that nobody noticed.  You were a great athlete, a military man, a person everybody looks up to, even me.  Now, as the Eidolon, I get to be that. Okay, maybe I did change the specifics of the mission a bit, but really, it’s still the same mission in the big picture.  Please, Barr, we can’t end 30 years of friendship like this.”
“We’re not ending anything, buddy.  This is just one more lesson I’m trying to teach you.  You ARE a hero. More importantly, you’re my hero. That’s the hero I’ll always remember, but it’s not the kind of hero you’re pretending to be now.”  With that, he gave Hal a warm bear hug and slid open the side door to the van.  “Now, get out of here.  You’ve got a date with destiny’s uglier, fatter sister.  Try not to get tongue-tied.”
Hal started to get out of the van, then stopped and took one last look at his oldest friend.
“I’ll be watching,” Barry said, “so for God’s sake, make us look good.”
Hal half-smiled and shut the door, slinking off towards the park in the cover of darkness.

Hal made his way through the night like a ghost wearing too much armor.  He entered Chessman Park from the East. Behind him, rows of strip malls stretched for at least four blocks, each one holding at least half a dozen businesses, mostly owned by local families.  Restaurants, shops, bars, barber shops, some turning off their lights for the night and preparing to go home, others just getting started.  Hundreds of people quietly went about their lives, spending their money, and were completely oblivious to what was about to befall the neighborhood.
For that matter, Hal had decided he was just as oblivious.
A group of approximately 40 people had assembled on the basketball court, ten of them were playing while the rest looked on.  In reality, it wouldn’t have seemed that odd, except that people were hardly talking to each other, and when they did, it was in low, expectant murmurs, and not the cheers and shouts he would have expected such a game to illicit.  He took a position behind a tree where he could keep an eye on the proceedings but still be far enough away that he would not be easily spotted.  He knew he was in the right place when he noticed four dozen scooters parked on the sidewalk around the court.  These were not the small candy-colored rentals he was used to seeing.  These were larger, industrial, beefy, and probably not at all street-legal.  Each was painted white.
He started to adjust the nipplecam and report in to Barry and Vicky, but then remembered he was a solo act now.
It felt weird not having those two wisecrackers in his ears.  For all his fears about being in the “X-Men Last Stand” or “Matrix Revolutions” phase of his career, where things had just gotten so overdone they had become boring, he hadn’t taken into account that he was, instead, in the self-doubting, emotionally bankrupt, stripped of his best assets “Dark Knight Rises” or “Iron Man 3” phase of being a super hero.  He decided he would kill to have Chris O’Donnell suddenly show up uninvited and overact his way through a speech about the value of partnership right now. 
“I’m actually surprised you showed up, Eidolon,” a voice behind him said.  Hal turned and found himself face to face with the Hierophant, flanked by four rather large henchmen.  “I must say, I never took you for one who would voluntarily walk into this little Gethsemane I arranged for you.  It almost makes me ashamed to tell you that you could have stayed away.  Your presence here changes nothing about what is to come.”
