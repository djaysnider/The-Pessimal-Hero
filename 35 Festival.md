Hal didn’t bother with the mask, or the armor, or his dignity. He was still wearing his worn-out sweats and a body covered in bruises when he borrowed Barry’s car and made the short trip to St. Leonard's.  By the time he arrived, six police cars and several ambulances had taken control of the scene.  Sirens in the distance told him more were on the way.
He spotted Samuel Hamilton-Smythe interviewing a group of people at an overturned bake sale booth.  It didn’t appear that very many people had left the scene.
An elderly man in a red checked shirt waved at him and walked over. “Young Hal! So good to see you, we were so sorry to hear about your mother's passing. If there’s anything you need--”
“Thank you, Mr. O’Donnell, it really means a lot.  What happened here?”
“Oh, it was those damned scooter kids.  About a dozen came riding through here and just tore everything up.”
“Was anybody hurt?  Where’s Father Fallon?”
“Plenty of busted hips from people falling, I’ll wager, but they didn’t actually attack any people directly.  Just vandalized the displays, booths, and cars.  I don’t think they even took any money,” Mr. O’Donnell scratched his chin as if it was helping him remember.  “Father Fallon was impressive.  Kept yelling scripture at them like a street preacher on Red Bull and chased a dozen of them down the block singing Rock of Ages right about the time the police arrived.  I don’t know if I ever saw him come back to give a statement or not.”
There was a screech of feedback, and a voice coughed on the public address.  “Is this thing on?  Hello?”  Hal recognized the voice instantly.  “This is The Hierophant speaking.  Have you had enough yet, citizens of Riverside?  Are you tired of being victims of everything from corporate greed, political special interests, and gangs of local hoodlums riding motorized scooters?  Crime, corruption, vandalism, all run rampant in your neighborhood, and still you do nothing.  Your twisted self-service local politicians and your ineffectual police force are powerless to protect you.  The time has come for you to take matters into your own hands.  Look at the example set by your champion, the Eidolon.  Follow his lead and pull your city back from the downward spiral it is trapped in before it’s too late.”
There was another screech of feedback.  Hal looked around for the Hierophant, but he was nowhere to be seen.  The announcement booth was overturned and sat empty.  A police officer was holding an old-fashioned-looking microphone, which should have been connected to the PA system, but the cord dangled a few feet beneath the stand, intentionally cut.  Other officers frantically pointed at the Church office, the home of the main PA control. Four of them drew guns and raced into the building.
“Tonight at sundown, your battle commences, and I, for one, would like to invite your champion, The Eidolon, to surrender himself to me there and face his judgment.  If he doesn’t, I promise you, there will be more than simply overturned raffle tables and library books.  This neighborhood will burn in the fires of Hades, and those who choose to sit by and let it happen will be the first to perish in those flames--”
The voice cut off abruptly with a click as the PA was switched off.  “It was a cassette recording,” he heard someone say courtesy of a nearby officer’s radio.  “Looks like he’s long gone again.”
