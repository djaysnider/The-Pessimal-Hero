The suit, tailored around a mannequin that was based on an exact physical copy of his body, fit nearly perfectly.  He had put on a bit of bulk in the weeks since the body casts were made, but the breathable fabric of the under-suit compensated for this with only slight adjustments needed.  He was more surprised at how light it felt, considering the amount of armor he was wearing.  In the end, it wasn’t something he would want to wear all day, and certainly not something he could take on and off easily by himself, but it was reasonably comfortable, hardly restricted his movement at all, and in low-light situations, would allow him to pass for a slightly overweight guy in a hooded trench coat.
“I’ve got video up,” Vicky said, admiring the back of her head on the monitor from where Hal’s nipplecam was pointing.  “Try audio.”
Hal inserted one of the earpieces into his left ear and left the other attached to the speaker in the hood.  This way, he could clearly hear his surroundings as well as any instruction from Vicky and Barry, even if the hood slipped off accidentally in the heat of battle.  “Check, check, check one two,” he said, over-emphasizing the CH sounds and enunciating his vowels.
“This isn’t Woodstock, Punkin’. Just speak naturally.”
“Sorry.”
“Five by five here.  Volume good for you?”
“Just fine,” Hal said.
“Oh, forgot to tell you about these little honeys Vicky and I threw together for you,” Barry said, holding up two long pipe-like objects with small control boxes -- each with a single red button -- attached by wire and affixed to Hal’s belt. He instructed Hal to extend his arms and carefully slid them through loops on the underside of his forearm where they stopped just shy of his elbow so as not to impede his ability to bend his arm.
“What are these things?”
 “I call them the Retha Specials.  I’ll tell you when to use them.”
Vicky whirled around. “Muffin, that is not what we decided to call them, that's not even---”
Barry held up a hand to silence her. “I renamed them,” he said and then turned to Hal. “Just don’t go pressing buttons until I tell you. Oh, and try not to get them bent up blocking with them.  It could be bad.”
Hal was starting to get concerned.  “Define ‘bad.’”
Barry thought about it for a moment.  “Yeah, we’d probably better keep that a surprise, too.  Don’t worry about it, though.  I’m sure everything will be fine.”
“Any other surprises you’re not telling me about?”  Hal asked.
“Yeah, a few,” Barry grinned. “We don’t want you to get bored out there, after all.”
“Sensible of you.”
Vicky had already pulled up a large map of the city and was overlaying a diagram of recent crime activity as well as the current position of police officers.  The police scanner played quietly in the background, but to Hal, this was nothing more than a bunch of codes and numbers.  Fortunately, his team had been doing their homework as well.  “Any idea where you want to start out, Mr. eye-roll-on?”
“That’s Eidolon,” Hal corrected her in his best superhero voice. “Someplace close, until I get the hang of all this,” Hal decided.
“Well, we’ve had a few muggings over at the strip mall on Adams and 25th.  That stabby taco stand[^fn1] should be closing right about the time you get there, so anybody hanging around is bound to be up to no good.  You can park at the bus station three blocks away.  Should be inconspicuous enough.”
Hal knew the taco stand she was talking about, and it was sufficiently stabby for their purposes. He thought about it for a moment, then nodded, turned to Barry, and gave a casual two-fingered salute.
“Ookla, ride!” Barry said as he left the command center.

[^fn1]:  Named after, but not to be confused with “Stabby Taco” on West Washington in Indianapolis.
