It was just after midnight when Barry and Vicky heard the garage door open upstairs.  The car was eerily quiet and made practically no noise pulling in, unlike the old Ford Focus.  Two minutes later, Hal had finally finished limping down the back steps and entered the basement.  His foot looked like a football stuck in an ill-fitting tennis shoe.
“Ooooh, I bet that was a bitch to drive home with,” Barry said, making his way to the bar to pull some ice out of the freezer.
For the first time, Vicky was actually concerned.  She had played it cool and detached earlier around Barry, just for the sake of keeping his head in the game, but like Barry, when she heard the second gunshot, she thought their fun and games had come to a premature end, and she actually found herself sorry for Hal.  “You better give him a stiff belt of something too,” she told Barry, then turned to Hal, “How’s the adrenaline?”
“Yeah, I probably won’t sleep tonight.  That was a lot more intense than I thought it would be.”
Barry came back with an ice pack, which he was surprised to find sitting in the freezer of the beer fridge.  It seemed Vicky was a lot more efficient than he gave her credit for.  “How close did he come to getting you?”
“Honestly, not very,” Hal admitted.  “He was shooting blind, and thanks to my lightning-fast reflexes, I’d already ducked for cover before he even finished turning.”
“Still, you were damned lucky, Punkin’” Vicky scolded him. “Look at those old, beat-up things.  Coldplay has more sole than those things.  Next time you’re going back to the boots.”
“That was some feat,” Barry said.  “He had to be pretty quick.  You say you dove for cover before he even got the shotgun on you?”
“Yeah, just like we practiced.”
Barry nodded in agreement.  “And that’s when you cracked your ankle on, what was it? A display stand you dove behind?”
“A shelf, actually,” Hal corrected him.
“Really,” Barry stroked his beard, “because from where I’m standing, it looks like you slipped on the remains of one of the snack cakes he’d blasted into oblivion a few seconds before.”
Hal's jaw dropped. “What?! How did you--”
Vicky was smiling ear-to-ear and pointing at the television in the corner.  “Awww, Punkin’, you made the overnight news!”
The television showed black-and-white surveillance camera footage from the convenience store robbery -- Hal writhing in cream-filling and limping to his feet with the shotgun.  Vicky grabbed the remote and turned off “mute.”
“-- At first, police discounted the stories and assumed the individual to be a member of a rival gang, but a string of citizens came forth and quickly set them straight.”  The news woman faded from view and was replaced by a pretty girl dressed in a waitress uniform that Hal recognized as Helen Spiros.
“I’ll admit, I was being nice to him at first because I thought he was just some crazy, homeless guy. After talking to him for a few minutes, though, I was convinced he was really serious about protecting the innocents and ridding the city of criminals,” Helen said. “He just had this air of righteousness about him ... and he smelled like expensive leather.”
“Told you she was into you, Hal!” Barry elbowed him.
Hal smirked and shook his head. “Wow, she read a lot into that.  We mostly just talked about how to pronounce Eidolon.”
“It was incredible,” a young boy was raving.  Hal recognized him as one of the youths from the park.  “First, he took on four members of the Avocado Gang that were going to kill us and single-handedly, without any weapons, took them all down.  They came at him and he just BAM!  POP!  He knew jujitsu, krav-maga, tae-quan-do, all of it.  They didn’t stand a chance.  Then another dozen showed up, and he just scoffed and beat every one of them as well.  I’ve never seen anything like it, even in the movies!”
Finally, the clerk from the convenience store appeared on the screen.  He was still covered in bits of chocolate and sponge cake.  “Well, I mean, I appreciate when customers step and help, but honestly, I’ve had about a dozen people try to rob me in the past year, so I don’t keep that much cash in the register, and with the security cameras, the cops usually pick ‘em up an hour later.  Still, good for this Eidolon guy, I guess.  Saved the cops some trouble.  Even though he risked his life for a hundred bucks worth of snack cakes.”
“Police, on the other hand, were less than thrilled.”  The newscaster said by way of voiceover as the image changed to a cop standing next to his squad car in the convenience store lot, lights still flashing, spoke into a half-dozen microphones.
“Honestly, it’s very reckless of this individual to take the law into his own hands, and our department has a zero-tolerance policy towards vigilantes.  Not only is the weaponry he displayed in the surveillance footage questionable, if not downright illegal, but he also runs a significant risk of liability for assault, battery, property damage, and intimidation.  Frankly, if he’s listening right now, I’d advise him to stop before either he or an innocent bystander gets hurt.”
Vicky turned the TV off.  “Don’t listen to him, Punkin’.  They have to say that kind of thing to the press or they’ll look bad.  Secretly, he’s just as glad he got some easy arrests tonight thanks to you.”
Hal wasn’t sure what to think.  On the one hand, it was nice to know that, like all legends, stories of his exploits had exploded into tall tales after a matter of just a couple of hours.  On the other hand, the point was to avoid public exposure and keep things anonymous.  He had a feeling his job was going to get harder because he got a bit eager this evening.
