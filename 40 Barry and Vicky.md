CHAPTER TWENTY
“Hey lady, is this seat taken?” Barry Chase slid into the booth at John Howard’s café and grabbed a menu. 
Vicky Montana took a sip from her milkshake. “I would have thought you’d be in Mexico by now.”
“I would have thought you’d be on an island in the Caribbean by now.”
“Hell, no. Though I was a little surprised when the Feds weren’t camped out on my porch with a pair of zip ties and a very boring warrant.”  Her demeanor turned deadly serious.  “I assume you watched it.”
“Yeah, and I’ve been following it for the past week.  I still can’t piece together what happened.  By all accounts, Hal was right there in the middle of it and was even working with the Police at one point, though I’m not sure if that part was retconned in after the fact by their PR team.  As near as I can tell, several people reported seeing him in action during the riots, but nobody saw him leave, and his body wasn’t among the casualties.  It’s like both he and the Hierophant just up and disappeared.”
“Well, I guarantee somebody knows what happened to him. I’ll do some poking around and see what I can find out for us.”
Barry shook his head. “You know?  I don’t want you to.  I don’t think I want to know.”
Vicky smiled. “You’re right. I don’t know what’s nicer, thinking he found his perfect death and died being the hero he wanted to be, or thinking maybe he’s still out there somewhere kicking back with a beer before he goes out on patrol again tonight.”
“So, it’s our secret pact then,” Barry said, holding up his pinkie. “Hal maybe died a hero, maybe didn’t. But whatever we find out---if we ever find out---we take it to our graves.”
Vicky matched pinkies with him and laughed quietly to herself.  “I am going to miss him, but I’m kind of glad it’s all over,” she said.
“I would have thought you enjoyed the chance to flex your muscles like you did.”
“Muffin, I was on the fast track to a cushy vice president job before your little friend decided he’d saved my ass in that very parking lot right there and recruited me as his cosplay seamstress and sidekick.  I was perfectly happy before that, and if I’m lucky, I’ll get to go back to being perfectly happy about that.”
“That’s the parking lot where you met Hal? I didn’t know that.  Very appropriate,” Barry smiled.
“What do you mean, you didn’t know?  Why did you ask me to meet you here, of all places, then?”
Barry’s eyes widened.  “I didn’t. You asked me.”  They both pulled their phones out at the same time and compared identical text messages.
“Technically, it was me,” a third voice said.  Barry and Vicky looked up to see that Special Agent Samuel Hamilton-Smythe had entered the café and was strolling, or was it more “shambling,” towards them.  He was wearing an eyepatch over his left eye, his right arm was in a sling, and he was walking with the assistance of a cane. “You’ll forgive me if I don’t sit down, I apparently took a 410 load of black powder and rock salt to my buttocks.  It’s not enough to get you a Law Enforcement Purple Heart, but it will get you teased mercilessly in the locker room.” He winced and grabbed a chair, which he sat backwards against the table and attempted to straddle.  Normally this would be considered a “baller move,” but the amount of wincing and squirming Smythe made reduced the scoring to somewhere between amusing and feeble.
Barry and Vicky looked at each other with concerned expressions.  Smythe allowed them to stew a little before cracking a warm smile. “Relax,” he said at last, “if I wanted to arrest you, I’d have texted, 'Free donuts at FBI headquarters,’ and just waited by the door for you to show up.”
“I wouldn’t be bragging too much, Boo-boo. You look like an ‘after picture’ for an auto insurance ad.”
Smythe held up a conceding hand.  “Fair. We weren’t exactly trained to defend ourselves against bass-heavy funk escapes and industrial smoke bombs. Let’s call it one-and-one between us.  I highly doubt LaCrosse over here came up with that escape plan.”
“No, but I do know how to make a concealable rock salt zip gun, so why don’t we just call it one-all as long as we’re keeping score?”
Smythe eyed Vicky, looking for confirmation.  “He’s good,” she begrudgingly admitted.
“I don’t suppose, and this would be completely off the record, you’ve heard from your third musketeer, Mr. O’Grady?”
“No,” Vicky answered quickly, “and we’ve decided we’re going to stop asking, so why are you?”
“Honestly, we were late arriving at Lakeside.  The police had him, but really they were rooting for him and had no interest in capturing, so whether they knew anything about his fate or not, we can’t say.”
“Look, if you’re going to go on a manhunt for Hal, there’s something you need to know.”
“About Weinrib-Živojinović Syndrome?  I already do,” Smythe paused, “and I’m sorry.”
“You knew?!” Barry asked, amazed.
“Not at first, we really didn’t know anything about your operation.  I only pieced most of it together over the last couple of days.  You see, when I told your friend I was part of a task force assigned to curtail vigilante activity, that wasn’t strictly true.  Actually, I’m recruiting.”
“Vigilantes!?” Vicky asked, amazed.
Smythe shrugged.  “More like, people with tactical expertise and connections who know how to get things done outside of the more constrained parameters of law enforcement.  Something between a bounty hunter and a superhero.  Why, do you guys know anybody who might have skills in those areas?”
“What’s it pay?” Vicky asked.
“More than the Vice President job we were lining up for you that will probably be rescinded very soon, considering your recent activities.”  
Vicky raised up. “You did NOT just try to blackmail me, did you, Boo-boo?  Because I can show you skills I never got to use with these guys.”
“Save it for your team, then,” Smythe smiled.
