“Back to basics,” it turned out, meant driving around aimlessly late on a Friday night looking for trouble  to appear.  Sadly, trouble seemed to have taken the night off.  Vicky and Barry sat passively in the man cave.  Barry was splitting his attention between a baseball game and the Roadster’s hood cam; Vicky was doing her usual diligence, listening to police scanners and monitoring what little she could get of police activity via the public API’s to the department’s dispatch center.    
“Why are all those screens blank?” Barry asked. “Did Hal forget to pay the cable bill?”
“I shut them down, Muffin.  After Double-Oh-No showed up, I thought it best to discontinue the 'less-legitimate' portions of our arsenal. The longer I can avoid tipping off Boo-Boo and the Department of Bad Acronyms, the more our boy can move around and do his thing.”
“And the longer you can put off the inevitable decision,” Barry said without accusation.
Vicky stared at him like a poker player looking for a tell.
“Hey, it’s okay,” Barry said, “I'm stuck here for altruistic reasons.  You have much more to lose than we do. I don’t think anybody would begrudge you if you decided to leave now.”
“Are you trying to get rid of me, Muffin?”
“Well, you have to admit, you’re the one who steered us off the path of noble sacrifice, into a low-budget Netflix thriller. Now we’re public enemies, the cops aren’t our friends, and the Department of Inner-city Logistics and Domestic Operations is probably tapping our phones. I just want to help my friend, not spend the rest of my life in a ten-by-ten room with a man who calls me his ‘Poodle.’”
“It’s the basic difference between you and me, Muffin. And it’s what Agent Zipline was trying to tell Hal. You’re doing this to make your friend happy. It’s a moral obligation to you. So, you’re predictable. I’m doing this because it’s the right thing. Innocent people are in trouble.  We are possibly the most knowledgeable, best-equipped, and most strategically placed team to help.  You and Hal did not want to face that, and as your partner, I respect that.  Hal is on the other side of town from that action.  That’s what we decided.  But, don’t think for a minute when this is all over, I am abandoning my responsibility to that other war you guys don’t want to fight, just same way I’m going to abandon my responsibility to the two of you.”
“Sounds like a very lonely life ahead of you fighting other people’s battles.”
“It beats a 9-to-5 job you don’t believe in.  The trick is to make their battles your battles.  That way you’re never alone, Muffin.”[^fn1] 
“Forget I said anything,” Barry said and turned back to his terminal.
“I usually forget everything you say, Muffin,” Vicky said and punched up the feed to the Roadster.  Hal’s face appeared on one of the monitors.  “How’s it look out there, Punkin’?”
“Quiet,” Hal said over the comm. 
“I’m showing a couple of noise complaints have been called in about a grocery store parking lot over on Fox Street.  That’s only a couple of miles east of you.  Best lead we’ve had so far.  I’m sending the directions to your GPS now.”  She expertly typed in a couple of commands and received a disappointing tiny chime.
“Got ‘em,” Hal confirmed and started up the Roadster, receiving four equally unrewarding chimes telling him to fasten his seat belt.
Barry was engrossed in the power of Google.  “That’s a really rough neighborhood. Heck, it’s Disneyland for the ankle-monitor crowd. They’ve had a serious number of robberies, fights, and assaults in the past couple of months.”
“No shootings?” Vicky asked.
“Doesn’t appear to be,” Barry confirmed.
“Well, that sucks.  Maybe Hal will get lucky and someone will escalate things.”
Ten minutes later, Vicky saw on the display that Hal had reached his destination.
“I can see the grocery store. Looks like some kind of protein-shake and mommy issues convention in the parking lot,” Hal said.  “We’ve got motorcycles, a couple of classic muscle cars, and about twenty members of the Tooth of the Month Club. They're rocking a Motorhead CD on one of the car stereos at an unsafe decibel level, so I’m going to guess this is the place to be for criminal activity tonight.  Circling back now.”
Barry turned on his microphone.  “Hal, are you really gonna drive a conspicuous $100,000 vehicle in front of a bunch of guys who would sell their grandmothers for beer money?”
Vicky chimed in, “Nuh uh, Punkin’. You’re going to park MY car right there and watch from a safe distance and watch quietly until something happens.”
“YOUR car?” Barry asked again, confused.
