Hal parked the Roadster three blocks away and made his way to the garage on foot, completely via back alleys.  Unless the racoons in the dumpsters were on someone’s payroll, he felt safe from detection.  He came upon the back door and reported to Barry and Vicky.
“Okay, I’m in the alley behind the building.  You’re right.  There are at least 30 scooters parked here, all three varieties.  Looks like a preschooler biker rally. There’s light and noise coming from inside the building.  The only first-floor windows are on the garage door and the exit door next to it.”
“Watch for someone guarding the door or patrolling the outside of the building,” Barry suggested.
“Not seeing anyone,” Hal confirmed.  “I’m going to keep walking down the alley like I belong here, then try to run up and get a look in the window when I get alongside it.”
“Solid plan,” Barry said.
The voices grew louder, but still undistinguishable as Hal continued walking down the alley behind the building.  When he was within ten feet of the back door, he quickly sidled over, put his back against the wall, and peeked over his shoulder inside. 
A flickering fluorescent tube lit the center area of the garage, not attracting attention from the front.  About 30 people aged 19 to 29, in Hal’s estimation, had scattered around the room and were talking among themselves in small groups.  About half were sitting on counters or benches.  In the center of the room, six people were hunched over a card table, looking at a series of what Hal could only guess were maps of some kind, or possibly pizza menus.  Even with his ear against the door, he couldn’t distinguish any of the voices, all of which were talking over each other.  Hall quickly turned his body and pressed his chest to the glass so the others could get a view of the room via the nipplecam.  
“What’s upstairs?”  Vicky asked.
“It’s just a loft with what looks like parts storage.   There’s a catwalk leading from that to the office in the far corner of the building, which I assume is where the stairs are.  The office window is dark. It doesn’t look like anyone’s in there.”  Hal quietly and slowly tested the nob on the door.  “This door is locked, so I’d have to go in through the front to get up there.  Doubtful I’d make it without being spotted.”
“Not necessary,” Barry said triumphantly.  “Google satellite view tells me you’re standing next to a ladder.”
Hal looked around.  Sure enough, there was a narrow metal ladder secured to the side of the building.  Directly over his head was a ventilation window that doubled as a fire escape.  “So I am,” he said, made sure the surroundings were clear, and started up the ladder.  Upon reaching the window, he confirmed the rows of shelves containing parts obscured the view from the shop floor, cracked the window a bit wider with his left arm, and slipped inside with the grace and stealth of a horse climbing out of a VW Beetle.
The floor was an iron grating, which unfortunately provided zero stealth.  Hal made his way to the far end of the loft hoping no one looked up.  As he peered over,, voices rose clearly from the table beneath him. 
“I dunno man.  I don’t see how rousting old people in a church parking lot is lucrative,” a man in a green t-shirt said.
The guy across from him in a baseball cap shook his head nervously. “Orders are orders, man.”
“Why don’t we send the noobs to do that while we hit the edge of campus, or better still the northside parking lots.”
“We could do that,” said a third man, covered in tattoos and wearing a white tank top.
“Man, do you wanna mess up this good thing we got goin’?” Baseball cap conveniently raised his voice.  “He told us Phase Two is three targets.  We do the library, Avocado does the church, and Streak does the park.  It’s simple. We can’t mess this up.”
The tattooed man pointed a finger at baseball cap. “Man, you should have made him give us at least half of the new hardware as a down payment.  We look like chumps.”
“I ain’t messin’ with this guy.  He’s pretty dialed in. He knew my name, my address, my cell number.  He’s connected somehow. “
“Yeah, mine too,” the one in the green t-shirt agreed. “Maybe should just trust him and follow instructions.”
“That’s messed up.  This had better pan out or we’re goin’ back to the mattresses,” the tattooed one said, throwing up his arms in frustration.
“What mattresses? You tired or somethin’?”
“You know, the mattresses.  As in, goin’ to war.”
“What does war have to do with mattresses?!
“Man, didn’t you ever read the Godfather?”
“Books are for nerds.”
“I’ll get you a library card.”
Vicky's voice came over the comm in Hal’s ear.  “Punkin’, we’re researching, but pretty much all every library in town is near a church and a park.  You gotta get a look at those maps.”
Hal said nothing but leaned forward over the railing to get a better look at the table.  Suddenly, with an ominous groan of old metal and cheap labor, the railing gave way, and before Hal could assess his life choices of late, he found himself clinging to it three feet above the assembled gang members all of whom were now looking up at him in a combination of terror and awe. 
He couldn’t believe his luck. He had somehow ended up hanging on with one arm, and needed only to deliver his big intro line, let go, and drop gracefully in the middle of the table, where a righteous ass-kicking would then ensue.
“Sorry, I’m late, but traffic was---"
He was cut off abruptly as the loft tore free of the wall, lurching forward and sending him plummeting past the table and into the crowd, where -- with what he hoped was a miraculously graceful front flip -- he landed in a crouching position, tucked into his momentum, did one roll, and came up on his feet, face to face with the tattooed gentleman, who turned out to be much bigger in person.
“---oh nevermind,” Hal said. 
“Holy crap, it’s HIM!” one of the gang members he shouted and pointed at him.  Hal recognized him instantly as the guy who ran away for reinforcements the weekend before.  “It’s Idolman! That guy who beat up Doug last weekend!”
Hal was debating whether to correct the thug or not when he Barry’s panicked voice over the comm.  “Dude, quit sitting around!  Right front pocket!” Hal smiled, reached in his pocket and pulled out two smoke bombs.  He tossed one politely to Tattoo Guy stopped midway through swinging a punch at Hal’s head to catch it and regard it with confusion. He lobbed the other over his head onto the fallen loft where the other large group had gathered.  He took off for the door while the smoke and a few lungfuls of phosphorus pentoxide were still sufficient cover.
He wasn’t quite fast enough and found his legs kicked out from under him after three steps. This time he was thankful for an actual attacker being the reason for his fall instead of cream filling. He was also thankful for quality footwear and ankle support.  With a quick elbow to the face and a heartfelt, "Excuse me, sorry,” he got his attacker to release him and scrambled to his feet, only to find his attacker desperately clinging to his leg again.  A second elbow, this time without the apology, made his assailant disengage.  In the end, it took a third elbow and smashing the guy’s face into the floor to make him reconsider his life choices.
By the time Hal reached the front door, the room was full of smoke.  Everyone was confused, panicked, and coughing too much to pay attention to him.  On his way out, he flipped off all the light switches and left the entire room coughing and choking in near total darkness.
He was around the side of the building and back in the cover of the dark alley when he heard the building’s smoke alarm go off.  “Police and Fire are three minutes away,” Vicky said in his ear, “Should be adequate cover.”  At the end of the block, he slowed to a walk, panting heavily.
“You’ve got to get yourself some stamina for running,” Barry teased him over the intercom.
“I hate these boots,” Hal moaned.
“They probably saved you another sprained ankle in that fall though.”
Suddenly, Hal heard the unmistakable sound of scooters revving up in the alley behind him.  “Oh, no, they got out, they’re mobile and after me.”  He started running again.
“What kind of scooters?” Vicky asked.
“What?  The two-wheeled things that look like skateboards with handlebars.  What do you mean what kind?”
“No, Punkin’. Avocado? Sparrow? Which service.”
“Hang on, I’ll go back and ask,” Hal said sarcastically. “Should I ask their sign and favorite color too?!”
“You keep running.  I got you.”
“You’ve got me?!”
“Yeah, just get back to the car as fast as you can.”
“I wasn’t going to stop for coffee!”
The scooters were closing rapidly.  Hal didn’t dare turn around to check, but he estimated no more than two or three paces behind him.  Suddenly, with several shouted expletives, the sound of bodies falling over each other, and the satisfying crunch of metal scooters tumbling through the air, everything stopped.  Hal kept running, and the voices kept getting more and more distant.
“Vicky, what did you do?!” Hal asked, completely confused.
“I’m still wired into all three of those rental companies’ networks.  I just did a diagnostic reboot, which shuts all the scooters down until they recover the GPS and report back in.” 
Hal was finding it difficult to laugh and run for his life at the same time.  “Brilliant!” was all he managed.  He didn’t stop running until he reached the street where he parked the Roadster.  As he rounded the corner, he nearly collided with a man in a long tan coat and crumpled brown suit.
“Mr. Eidolon,” he said calmly.
“Who the hell are you?!” Hal asked?
The man pulled a business card out of his breast pocket and held it out for Hal.  “Special Agent Samuel Hamilton-Smythe, Department of Inner-city Logistics and Domestic Operations.  I’ve been looking forward to meeting you.”
