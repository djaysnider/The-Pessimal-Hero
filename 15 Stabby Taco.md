It was the calm before the storm, Barry knew.  Between finding a parking spot and walking the last three blocks, it would take at least 25 minutes for Hal to reach the stabby taco stand and his dance with destiny.  Barry paced for the first five and pretended to study Vicky’s screens for the second five. He tried to play a game of Asteroids after that but found he simply could not concentrate.  Finally, he gave up, walked over to the wet bar, and grabbed a beer out of the fridge.
Vicky, who had been dozing peacefully for the last few minutes, popped one eye open and stared at him when she heard the can crack open.  “You seriously gonna drink at a time like this?”
“Hey, I’m not the one in the suit.  I just watch all these screens.”
“Nuh uh, Muffin,” Vicky said, shaking a finger at him.  “You watch THAT screen.” She pointed to a laptop sitting by itself on a narrow desk in the corner, facing 90 degrees away from her wall of technology.  
“Why ‘Muffin,' by the way?” Barry asked.
Vicky smiled.  “Because you're warm like a muffin. You're soft like a muffin. And you're easily baffled by discussions with big words in them ... like a muffin.”
“Fair,” Barry said as he took his seat and stared at the sleek black laptop in front of him. It's keys glowed and pulsed in an angry red color, and the bright screen showed the infamous Google splash page.
Barry regarded the laptop with disappointment. “What am I supposed to do with this?”
“Whatever I tell you to,” she scolded.
He took a couple of sips of beer half-heartedly and adjusted the angle of the screen even more half-heartedly.
“Oh, don't you worry about him, Muffin.  He'll be fine.”
“He literally wants to get himself killed.  He wants me to help him. I was dumb enough to go along with it up to this point.  Now it looks like he just might pull it off.  How can I not be worried about him?  I'd feel better if he really was a superhero. He's just a pile of unresolved issues and a billowy trench coat. Noir is not a superpower.”
“He’ll do fine.  Did you see the look in his eyes?  He’s having the time of his life.  I think he feels like he has a purpose for the first time. He’s not gonna get himself killed the first night.  Besides, the survival instinct is a lot harder to suppress than you two think it is.”[^fn1]
“He’s always had a purpose.”
“You know that.  I know that.  People like him can’t see the forest for the trees, though.  They feel burdened by responsibility and always having to be the reliable one, but that’s their gift.  He thinks because he’s finally getting to do his own thing, he’s finally fulfilling his purpose, but all he’s done is left one responsibility for a new one and one master for another.  When he figures that out, THEN he'll be ready, and we can say goodbye and mean it.   Until then, you keep being a good friend and sticking by him the way you have.”
Barry nodded but said nothing.  
Vicky broke the uncomfortable silence. “So, out of curiosity, what superpower do you think Hal should have?”
Barry shook his head. “I guess he doesn't need superpowers. He's got a support team, preparation, tactics, and a small arsenal under that coat.”
Vicky shook her head. “Zip ties, a flashlight, and a granola bar are not a tactical arsenal.  They're barely date night for me.”
“He's also got smoke bombs, flashbangs, stink bombs--”
“Muffin, if he needs smoke bombs to get out of a situation, he's no vigilante, he's a damned magician.”
“Oh, so by that logic, Batman is just David Copperfield with unresolved parental issues?”
“Batman would be the first one of those guys to go down if he didn't have plot armor.”
“Batman with prep time beats Superman.  It's canonical.” Barry was starting to lose his patience.
“So, you're saying Punkin’ needs a PowerPoint and severe neurosis?”  Vicky laughed, gesturing at the whiteboard.
“Either way, with Batman, it's not about the powers. He's a hero because he does the job that nobody else will do, just like Hal.”
“Muffin, so does the guy who hoses out port-o-lets for a living.”
 They both sat in silence for another five minutes.
“Which brings up a question,” Barry said, spinning his chair around. “Hal and I have been best friends almost all our lives.  So, it’s pretty obvious why I’m going along with this.  You, on the other hand, had only met him once.  So, why are you here?  And don’t give me that crap about how he saved your life from those muggers.  I know you well enough to know which of you two was the bigger threat to those guys, and I know him well enough to dissect his version of truth from what probably really happened.”
Vicky snickered, got up, grabbed a beer for herself, and began pacing the room.  “Maybe I just like helping people.  Maybe I just miss being a mamma.”
Barry nodded. “Yeah, I think that’s part of it.”
“Oh, so now you’re Mister Hannibal Lecter over there, dissecting people by reading their body language or something?”
Barry took a swig of beer and went back to studying the Google logo again, noting that it was International Yellow Pig Day.  
“I had a boy once.  A lot like Hal,” Vicky said, slumping back into her chair.  Barry slowly turned back around to face her.  She had a look of deadly seriousness.  “He was a good boy.  Did well in school.  Liked electronics and gadgets, like his mamma.”  She took a long gulp of beer.  “I wanted to set a good example, so when he was old enough to be on his own, I went back to school, worked two jobs at the same time.  Studied my ass off.  Eventually, I graduated and got a really good job as a programmer.  Turned everything around for us.  Only, when I finally did, he wasn’t there anymore.”
Barry said nothing.  He was finding it harder to keep the woman’s gaze.
“I had been gone so much and so busy with myself, I didn’t even notice he held it against me.  I didn’t even notice when he’d fallen in with a bad crowd and was staying out late at night doing who knows what. Jacking cars. Robbing people. Worse?”  She closed her eyes and shook her head.  “Anyway, turns out one night, he got in over his head and shot an innocent girl.  I don’t know if he knew her, or if he was robbing her, or if he accidentally shot her while aiming at someone else.  By the time the gangs were done with whatever little spat they were having that night, eight boys were dead, including mine.”
“Vicky, I’m so sorry.”
“That’s not the half of it,” she interrupted.  “You see, when the cops told me what my boy had done to that girl, I was more upset at her death than his.  As soon as he did that, he got what he deserved.  But that girl, now that’s on me.  She was innocent, and my boy took that from her.  I should have seen what was going on with him.  I should have seen that I had driven him out there because I was too wrapped up in myself.  He paid his price for her death, but I haven’t paid mine yet.”[^fn2]
“Hey, you can’t blame---”
Vicky pointed her finger straight at Hal. “Muffin, I know what the rest of that sentence is, and don’t you dare say it.”
Barry looked sheepish and took another sip of beer.
“Now,” Vicky continued, “maybe you’re right, and you probably are, but that doesn’t change how I feel in my heart.  When Punkin’ told me what he was planning, I took that as a sign from God, himself.  He is now my surrogate son. He wants to save an innocent life, and even though he didn’t know anything about me or that story I just told you, he came to me and asked me to help him do it.  Now tell me there isn’t some divine chess playing going on here to make that happen.”
There were a million flaws in her argument, but Barry couldn’t bring himself to point any of them out.

[^fn1]:  I threw this exchange in because I wanted to diffuse some of the tension and assure the reader, and Barry, that we are going to have at least some fun and games before going back to dark comedy.

[^fn2]:  Vicky’s backstory is a throwback to the original dark comedy draft. I think it really brings down the story, but on the other hand, it totally gives Vicky an unbreakable crucible to keep her in the story and explains why she doesn’t walk away at the first sign of trouble. So, I like it, I think it works, but it’s soooo grim. I don’t know what to do with it.
