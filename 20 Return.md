Back at the command center, Vicky threw her hands up in frustration. “Was that really necessary?” she protested with a hint of real anger in her voice. “You could have scratched up my car!”
“YOUR car?!” Barry asked from the chair next to her.
“Oh.” She caught herself and smiled genially. “Never mind, Muffin.”  
Barry looked over at the screen between them, which now showed Hal, grinning from ear to ear as he sat in the driver's seat of the Roadster.  “What now? Had enough excitement for one night yet?”
“I’m just starting to get comfortable,” Hal said. “Testing my legs, so to speak. Starting small and building up my confidence and all.”
“Well, you better make yourself scarce,” Vicky said, watching a readout of crime reports in the city.  “Somebody just made a 911 call from an address just up the street.  Code 415, assault with a weapon.  Your confidence is going to be all over the police bands in about 15 minutes.”
Barry rolled his eyes. “As what? Cosplaying Jedi stabs scooter roughnecks and runs away.”
“She’s right.  I’m heading back to base,” Hal said, sounding somewhat disappointed.
Barry flipped off the camera and mic from their side, though he left their view open.  “So what exactly is going on here?”
“What do you mean, Muffin?”
“Don’t ‘Muffin’ me.  We’re way off mission here.  This was supposed to be a somewhat twisted ‘assisted suicide’ for our friend here to find him an honorable death.  How was picking a fight with a group of what were probably just kids, in any way supporting that noble cause?”
“I’m just helping our friend, Muffin. He’s got to find his own way to whatever it is he wants to do. What do you want me to do, steer him into a gang shootout on his second night and hope for the best?”
Barry held her gaze but said nothing.
“More than anything, right now, that boy wants to feel useful,” she continued.  “He did good tonight. Maybe not ‘life-saving’ good, and maybe not even the morally upstanding version of good, but I don’t have much sympathy for people who pick on the innocent, and neither does he.  He’s still not ready to die.  He hasn’t seen it yet, but tonight was all that anger in him finally coming out.  He's mad about dying, mad about the limited time he has left to make something of his life, but most of all he’s mad about all the unresolved stuff from the past 35 years -- every bully he failed to stand up to; every time he could have said something but didn’t.  He’s cleaning his conscience of every action he didn’t take and then spent years playing it back in his head, saying, ‘If only I’d done this ... instead of that.’ When he’s done with all that, then he’s going to have nothing left but to face what’s really hurting him, but then he’ll be ready to do it on his own terms.  It’s up to us to help him get there and be there for him when he needs us.  Now, you think I did the wrong thing?”
There was a long pause.  “No, I guess not.  Hell, what’s the right thing in a situation like this?  It’s insane, and he’s dragged us into it.  I just ...” he stopped, not able to find the right words.
“You’re his best friend, but you have your own truths to face as well.  You just want this all over with, so you don’t have to worry about him every night for the next six months.” She gave him a very maternal pat on the leg.  “And you feel guilty because you think that means you wish he’d die sooner rather than later.”
“Yeah,” Barry said with a half-laugh. “Pretty messed up if you ask me.”
“Tell me what part of this whole thing isn’t messed up, Muffin.”
A signal on one of the monitors began beeping.  Vicky suddenly sat bolt upright, her eyes alight.  She flicked the mics and cameras back on.  “Punkin', tell me you bought the charging pack when you conned that guy out of the Idlolmobile this morning.”
“Charging pack?!” Hal asked.  “No, I just assumed ... I mean ... isn’t there a cable you plug into the wall or something?”
“You didn’t even look, did you?”
Hal frowned. “I didn’t even look.”
“Well, you’re down to ten miles, and you’re fifteen miles from home, Punkin’.”
Hal let out an expletive. “How was I supposed to know that?  The stupid car could have warned me.”
“Yeah, why didn’t it?” Barry asked.
Vicky punched mute on their microphone. “Well, probably because we took over his onboard monitor to show him a live video feed of his own basement.”  She gestured at the screen that he should have been seeing that had just alerted them to the power situation.  She clicked a few buttons and sent the screen to Hal’s terminal.
“What’s this?!” He asked.
“Oh, that?” she asked, innocently acting as if it had been there unnoticed the entire time.  “That’s telling you where the nearest supercharger station is. Should take you about 15 or 20 minutes to get enough charge to get you home.”
“You’ve got to be kidding me!”
“Lesson learned, Punkin’.”
