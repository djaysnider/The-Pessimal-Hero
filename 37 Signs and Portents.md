“I don’t know Barr.  This one has that final boss vibe about it,” Hal said somberly.
“Don’t talk like that man.  You’re letting that guy get into your head.”
“You don’t feel it?  It’s like everything has been building to this.  This is the General Zod vs Superman fight they teased in the origin movie.  It’s Kirk vs Khan.  It’s Luke vs Vader.  Where can you go from that?  The next movie is always some deconstruction storyline where the hero either loses his power in an unfamiliar setting or battles a metaphorical representation of his inner demons -- or worse, Richard Pryor.  I can’t do camp comedy, Barr.  One way or the other, I have to end this tonight.”
Barry snapped a flap closed on Hal’s chest piece and shook his head. “Whatever happened to ‘save one life’? Huh? What about doing the right thing even if nobody notices? What did I tell you, way back when you started this?”
“That I’d tear a ligament trying to jump off a dumpster in costume?”
“Well, yeah. But also, you don’t pick your own ending. You try to script your exit, you’re just screw up overthinking and make everything worse.”
“Okay, maybe you were right, but what else can I do at this point?”
“What you need to do is get your head on the mission.”  Barry slid an armor plate into position and fastened the flaps to secure it in place.  “Save people. Stop The Hierophant. If possible, avoid the authorities. You get those three taken care of, then you cue the Rachmaninoff, but not a minute before.”  He stopped suddenly and stared Hal right in the eyes. “Or, just stop. You’re not under any obligation to do any of this.”
“You heard the speech.  He’s threatening violence if I don’t surrender to him.”
“Is he tough?  This guy is obviously psychotic, but I’ve also heard street mimes that sounded more convincing. He might just want an audience. You don’t show up? Maybe he short-circuits and throws a tantrum. You do show up? Maybe he starts hurling flaming dumpsters at Girl Scout troops. Either way, we’re not qualified for this crap. We know the time and place---let the pros handle it.”
“But if I’m right, and I created this problem, then it becomes my moral imperative to fix it.”
“So, what are you going to do, go and beat the guy up? Knock him senseless and hope that’s enough to stop his grand plan? That doesn’t sound very heroic.”
“I won’t know until I get there and assess what’s going on.  At that point I’ll make the decision whether or not to call in the van.”
Vicky sat bolt upright in her chair.  “Uh, Muffin, speaking of vans. Is yours parked on the street out front?”
Barry shook his head. “Nope. Two streets back, like you told me. Hal’s driveway’s supposed to stay subtle.”
“Yeah, well,” Vicky said, pointing to the security feed, “subtle’s dead.”  The view showed Hal’s front yard and driveway from the point of view of a security camera on the corner of the roof.  Three large sedans and two vans had taken up position on both sides of the street across from them.
“Oh, no,” Hal’s eyes widened.  “Please, tell me that’s not the cops.”
Vicky shook her head, “It’s not the cops.”
Hal started to exhale in relief but caught himself, stopped, and regarded her out of the corner of his eye, waiting for the next shoe to drop.
“It’s the Feds,” she confirmed.  “Our boy Sammy is in the lead car checking his cell phone.  You got your keys, Muffin? We’re going to make a hasty exit.”
“Just like that?” Hal asked.
“Don’t you worry, Punkin’, Auntie Vicky was expecting this and has done her prep work.  Just get your stuff together and get in the laundry room.”  She reached up and pulled a jump drive out of the computer in front of her.
Barry raised a finger, “Wait, you didn’t safely eject that before---”
Vicky pursed her lips and shot him a look that would stop traffic. “Don’t start with me, Muffin.”  She reached in her pocket, pulled out a second jump drive and inserted it where the first one had been.  “Okay, they’re exiting the cars.  We’ve got about two minutes.” Instantly, every computer and piece of equipment in the room went dark. 
Hal was wearing half a costume and carried a small duffel bag that clanked with the armor plates he had not yet assembled into the suit.  He followed Barry through the now-hidden door that separated the command center from the laundry room.  Vicky followed twenty seconds later, wearing a gas mask and looking like the cover of Home Invasion Monthly.  She gave each of them a similar mask and extended a hand full of foam earplugs. “You’re gonna need these,” she said and started scrolling through apps on her phone.
Hal and Barry hastily put the masks on and inserted the earplugs. They all looked absolutely ridiculous, Hal decided.
“They’re positioned outside the garage, the front door, and the basement door.  Wait for my signal, and then run like hell out of the basement door, then straight across the back yard, through the neighbor’s yard, and over to Barry’s van.  Don’t look back, don’t stop until you get there.  If anybody trips or gets caught, we don’t come back for them.  We just keep running like hell until we’re in the van.”
Barry had already pulled the keys and remote starter out. “Well, unless I don’t make it, in which case you’re going to get about a block before the van shuts down.”
“You’re gonna make it, Muffin. Don’t you worry.” Vicky seemed unusually calm, as if she’d done this several times before.
CRASH.
The sound of all three doors being kicked in echoed through the house.
“FEDERAL AGENTS! GET ON THE GROUND!”
Silence.
Then more silence.
And finally, “There’s nobody here!”
“Basement’s empty!”
“Looks like the operation was down here...”
That was Vicky’s cue. She gave a sharp nod and tapped a button on her phone.
BWAAAAAAMP.
A screech erupted from the Marshall stack. It was like a horse screaming into a megaphone while someone tuned a car engine in C minor.
Hal winced even with the earplugs. “WHAT IS THAT?!”
Vicky grinned. “’The Grunt,’ J.B.’s. Featuring the immortal Bootsy Collins. You’re welcome.”
The noise turned rhythmic. The initial chaotic noise fading into funky bass that made the walls rattle, squealing sax that could shatter windows, and just enough chaos to make using the radios nearly impossible. The room filled with smoke. The lights were out. Outside the door, agents were shouting, tripping over gear, and preparing to file PTSD claims.
Thumbs up. Door open. Go time.
Barry was out first, knocking over an unlucky agent as if he were made of papier mâché. Hal and Vicky followed closely behind. The three tore through the backyard, hurdled the neighbor’s hedges, and crossed the street, masks flapping and adrenaline pounding.
“It’s parked at the next corner!” Barry yelled, hitting the remote start. The headlights of his white Ford Transit blinked on.
Barry and Hal dove in. Five seconds later, Vicky jogged up, out of breath but still managing to look smug.
“GO,” she panted, waving them off.
“GET IN!” Hal shouted, holding the side door open.
Vicky shook her head. “I’m not going. Without my gear, I’m dead weight. And more importantly, I’m the one they want.  Remember, I’m the one who stepped out of line and betrayed his trust.  He’s got nothing on the two of you. I’m the one he wants. That means I’m the one he’s going to be coming after.  You don’t need my tired ass slowing you down.”
Hal shook his head, “I’m not just going to abandon my friends, I’ll explain this whole thing was my idea---”
“Pfft!” Vicky stifled a real laugh. “They’re not going to buy that.”  Seeing the look of profound sadness on Hal’s face, she gave him a hasty hug.  “Don’t you worry about me. I eluded these guys for years. I can do it again. You go be a hero and forget all about old Vicky.”
Hal thought about it for a moment, then nodded in defeat. “Just make sure they don’t catch you.”
“Don’t you worry,” Vicky said, pointed a judgmental finger at Barry as an informal ‘Goodbye,’ and headed down the street towards her own car.
Hal shut the door just as Barry stepped on the gas.  The van pulled away at a casual, non-felonious speed.  Nothing to see.  Just two guys and a bag of armor plating and personal explosives, rolling toward a final confrontation with madness for the soul of the city. They didn’t look back at the agents storming Hal’s house behind them.
