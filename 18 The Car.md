Saturday evening at 6 PM, Hal strode into the man cave/command center all smiles while whistling a chipper tune.  Vicky and Barry were already setting up camp and reviewing the nipplecam footage from the previous night.  A still image of two of the punks was displayed on one of the large television screens that served as monitors.
“What’s that all about?” Hal asked.
“Well,” Barry began, “We only had a few good frames showing the faces of those bad guys from last night.  Vicky here is trying to run it through facial recognition in hopes we get a match.”
“They’re purse snatchers,” Hal protested. “Why are we trying to identify them?”
“Best possible use of our time,” Vicky said.  “If we can figure out who they are, we may be able to figure out where they hang out. Then, you can be there before them and maybe catch them in the act.  The odds are that they do this kind of thing every night.  Why not send you to where we know criminals are rather than have you wandering around looking for trouble?  Besides, can you imagine the look on their face when they realize the Idolman has singled them out for his fists of justice?”
Hal rolled his eyes, then nodded in half-agreement. “Okay, put that way, it makes sense.”
Barry walked to the fridge and grabbed two beers.  “On the other hand, we’ve got both the home and work addresses for one Miss Helen Spiros,” he said tossing one to Hal.  “You know, if you want to take the night off and maybe reap the rewards of the superhero lifestyle.”
“Crime doesn’t take the night off for hanky panky, Barr.  Besides, I’ve got some new stuff to try out tonight.  Wanna see?” Hal gestured for them to follow him out the door and up to the garage.  Barry and Vicky exchanged curious glances and followed.
There, sitting in all its glory under a fluorescent bulb, was what looked to be a cross between a low-riding car and a stealth fighter.  “I’m thinking of having it painted gray to match the motif, but honestly, black is much more practical for night travel.” Hal smiled like a cat that had just feasted on both Tweety and Foghorn Leghorn.
“What the hell is that, Punkin’?” Vicky made a curious face.  “Looks like Darth Vader’s vacuum cleaner.”
“Dude, that’s an Avanti Roadster!” Barry almost screamed with excitement.
Hal nodded, pretending to be bored.  “Yeah, I know.”
“Whose is it?”
“It’s mine.  I bought it this morning.”
“How?!  These things are back-ordered with the dealerships.  It takes months to get hold of one, assuming your credit is good enough!”
“I went to the country club this morning and bought it off a crypto bro.”
“A crypto broker just sold you his car out of the blue like that?!”
“Well, he needed a bit of coaxing.  I may have ‘gently implied’ I just flipped my L1 NFT DAO token on Shiva MoonCat and cashed out because a rugpull was coming, but honestly, I think he was happy to be out from under the payments.”
“When did you learn to speak ‘Crypto bro?’”
Hal held up his phone and tapped his earbud. “Google Translate.”
“Damned expensive waste of money if you ask me, unless your pen is out of ink, that is.” Vicky said opening the door and examining the dash.
Hal furled his eyebrows, “We used his pen to sign the title over.”
“No, Punkin’. The metaphorical pen.  This car is made for big generals, to make them feel  better when the little general stops taking orders. Get me?”
“Oh, yeah, I get it,” Hal said.  He did not.
Vicky whistled as she examined the interior, which, in addition to the standard steering wheel and gear shift, also had a large computer monitor on the passenger side dash with two smaller displays in the middle.  “Oooh, but we can work with this.  Let me put out a couple of queries with my shadier friends and get the full specs.  Muffin, can you mount a couple more of those cameras, left mirror, right mirror, facing outward, and then get us something for the inside?”
“On it!” Barry said, still excited.  He paused at the door. “Hey, do you have a name for this bad boy yet?  Like the Eidolmobile or something?”
“Nuh-uh, Muffin.  He’s done naming things.”
“Fine,” Barry said and exited.
“Now,” Vicky said scoldingly, “You don’t think maybe this bad boy is going to be too subtle, do you?  I mean, I thought we were going with a ghost motif here.”
“Being electric, it runs stupidly quiet. It’s black, so it’s hard to get a good look at it in the dark, and if we cover up the Avanti logos, it’s an uncommon enough car that anyone trying to identify it to the authorities would easily mistake it for a Corvette or a Viper or something.”
“Still not selling me.” Vicky shook her head.
“It has its own Wi-Fi hotspot and onboard computer, so you can easily tie it in with the command center downstairs and use the onboard terminal to send me updates, data, and whatever I need.  Plus, the check engine light doesn’t come on every time I start it.”
Vicky thought for a moment.  “Good, but still not good enough.”
“Fine, you can have it when I’m gone.”
She smiled. “That’ll do.”
Within two hours, Vicky had the complete specifications of the vehicle and had patched it into the command center’s network. This gave her complete telemetry on the car, access to the on-board computer and displays, and thanks to the new side cameras Barry had added, a complete 360-degree view of the car’s surroundings.
Hal sipped at his beer and admired the displays, currently showing the contents of the garage in more absurdly high detail than he ever wanted to see it. “Okay, so where are we fighting for justice tonight?” he asked.
Vicky tapped a few keys, and a vector map of the city popped up. She overlayed a heat map of color-coded clouds, which Hal recognized as a weather forecast for morally questionable activity.

“Well, police activity is a bit heavy in last night’s location,” she said. “So that’s going to complicate things. Not to mention, it puts a real damper on any quality criminal behavior. If it were me, I’d say that northwest of downtown by the old brass factory is your best bet. It’s been a hotbed the last few nights. Lots of reports of shady vans, suspicious trench coats, and one guy trying to pawn a stolen blender at 2 AM.  Plus, police coverage is thin tonight, thanks to the city using half the force for traffic control at the big game downtown.”
“Works for me,” Hal said, draining the rest of his beer. He stepped onto the suit-up platform and began slipping on his undersuit.  Barry held up the combat boots expectantly.
Hal recoiled as if they were radioactive. “I’m ditching the combat boots,” he announced, as if declaring independence from arch support. “I can’t protect the innocent with blisters on my feet. I need to lighten the load, and they’re the most obvious thing that can go.” He held up a pair of gray Sketchers. Or rather, what had once been off-white Sketchers before he’d assaulted them with spray paint.
Barry raised a skeptical eyebrow. “You sure that’s a good idea? There’s a reason soldiers don’t wear cross-trainers into combat, you know. Pretty sure nobody’s ever yelled ‘tactical advantage!’ while rocking memory foam.”
“They’ll have to do,” Hal said with all the conviction of a man ignoring decades of military footwear wisdom. “Also, I’m modifying the look a bit.” He slipped on a red neck gaiter, pulling it up to cover the lower half of his face. He struck a pose and held out his arms for feedback like a child demanding praise.
Barry squinted. “Oh, nice one, Shredder.”
There was a brief silence as everyone stared at the sad, spray-painted Sketchers.
“You look like a Soviet gym teacher who moonlights as a vigilante,” Vicky said. “And not a successful one.”
“Justice doesn’t need ankle support,” Hal shot back, hopping on one foot and nearly losing balance putting them on. “Justice needs speed. Agility. And a neck gaiter to catch my own heroic breath.”
“What if somebody unmasks you?” Barry asked.
“They’d probably apologize to him,” Vicky offered, one eyebrow raised.
Hal secured the last piece of gear and turned toward the exit hatch. “Tonight, evil meets orthopedic comfort.”
“Try not to trip on your way to righteousness,” Barry called after him. “And remember: run heel to toe!”
