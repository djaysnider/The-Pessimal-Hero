Hal sat bored in the Roadster, observing the grocery store parking lot for nearly half an hour. It was like a wildlife documentary about urban troublemakers. Customers nervously darted in and out of the store, constantly harassed by loitering miscreants. Hal wondered why anyone would willingly shop in a place that felt like the Escape from New York in grocery store form.
His existential musings about urban decay were interrupted by a woman's shrill scream. "Finally!" Hal shouted enthusiastically, quickly realizing how that sounded. He spotted two oversized goons shoving a young woman into a car at gunpoint, the tires squealing dramatically as they sped away. He quickly started his own engine and thrust himself out of his parking space to pursue them.  “Armed carjacking!” he screamed at Vicky.  “Time to see what this thing can do.”
Vicky had already sprung into action, getting a look at Hal’s location from the GPS, pulling up a map of traffic cams in his path, and sending their feeds to the larger monitors in front of her.  “Did you get a look at the car?  What kind is it?”
There were a few seconds of silence before Hal answered. “Erm.  Green.  I really don’t know anything about cars, Vicky.”
Vicky threw herself back in her chair.  “You’re a man, and you don’t know anything about cars?”
“He’s a Jr. High girls volleyball coach,” Barry offered. “He’d barely recognize a naked woman either.”
On the feed from the Roadster’s front camera, they could barely make out the car in question. It tore through a red light at nearly 50 miles per hour, causing cars to slam on their brakes and skid sideways to avoid colliding with it.  Hal cruised through right behind it, taking advantage of the now-stopped traffic.
“He probably spotted you as soon as you ran that light with him,” Barry offered.
Hal heard two faint cracks and saw the unmistakable muzzle flash of a pistol from the passenger side window.  They were shooting at him.  Nobody had ever shot at him before.  For a moment, he was terrified but then remembered that the entire goal of the mission was for people to shoot him, and preferably succeed. Tonight might be the night after all. Unfortunately, it was nearly impossible to hit a moving target from a moving target.  Hal had no idea where the bullets had landed, but they didn’t appear to have hit the car at all.
“They’re shooting!” Hal said excitedly.
Barry’s heart sank for a moment. “Uhhh. Congratulations, I guess?”
Vicky shook her head.  “That plus the traffic cam feed is gonna bring in a whole lotta police cars any minute now.  I don’t suppose you even had a plan when this started, did you?”
Hal shook his head.  “I was thinking something like, ‘run them off the road and then either disable or lead them away from the civilian?’”
Barry nodded. “Sounds solid.”
“Crossing 21st Avenue,” Vicky reported. “I see the car from the front video camera now.  That’s a 2013 Ford Fusion, for future reference.”
“So, they’re desperate enough to steal, but picky enough to avoid joyriding,” Barry said.
Vicky chuckled. “License number JB6527.  I’m updating the police with that information, so hopefully they’ll keep your attention on those guys and not you.”
The cars sped on, leaving the residential part of town for a small industrial park.  Hal checked the speedometer.  He was doing approximately 95, which didn’t seem all that fast in the Roadster at least, though when he came to a slight incline with a set of train tracks at the top, he was pretty sure he went airborne just a few inches.  The car landed again, the frame hitting the pavement with a crack that set his teeth on edge.
Vicky and Barry’s faces were replaced with a map of the industrial park. “He’s cornered himself,” Vicky said.  “The only exits from this park are on the east side.  I’ve marked them on the map.  If you turn right here, you can beat him to either of them.”
“Copy that,” Hal said as he slammed on the brakes and made a hard right between two warehouses in a direct line towards the northernmost of the two exits, assuming the carjackers would turn back south and try to use that one first.  He slammed on the brakes, turned the car sideways, classic action-movie style, and blocked the entire intersection in front of the exit. The front of the car was now facing South in case they still got past him, and he needed to get back in pursuit quickly.
Within seconds, headlights appeared up the road from the direction he assumed the carjackers would be coming from, and five seconds later, a 2013 Ford Fusion slammed on its brakes and, with a fury of screeching tires, slid diagonally to a stop almost directly next to the Roadster.
Instantly, Hal saw both car doors open and two men got out, one still carrying a handgun.  Hal opened his door and ran for the car as three bullets whizzed through his passenger window and embedded themselves in the side of the door.  Instinctively, he ducked. It was his closest call since becoming the Eidolon. Wait, why was he ducking?
He charged forward, slipped on his own bravado, and landed face to kneecap with the driver of the car, holding a knife of some sort.  Before the one with the gun could get around the car, Hal leaped on the driver, using the armor of his left forearm to block the knife until it slipped away when the two hit the pavement.  Hal quickly incapacitated the man with a palm thrust to the chin, sending his head back down to the pavement a second time. He followed with a swift knee to the groin for good measure.
Hal leapt back to his feet and faced the other carjacker. A gun was pointed straight at his chest. He hadn’t even had time to decide if this counted as a good enough death.  He simply closed his eyes and thought, “Be brave, be brave, be brave...” 
A strange crackling noise interrupted his near-death experience. Hal peeked to see Gun Guy twitching violently, collapsing as Special Agent Smythe dramatically entered with a taser.
"Dammit, Smythe!" Hal yelled. "You just ruined a perfectly good death!"
Smythe shrugged nonchalantly. "I warned you about this superhero nonsense. Coming quietly?"
"Are you finally arresting me, or is this another empty threat from Agent Ego?"
Smythe sighed wearily. "You WANT me to arrest you forcibly?"
"I prefer no arrest at all," Hal admitted. "But your obsession makes it seem inevitable."
"Fine," Smythe snapped, lunging awkwardly into a tackle so poorly executed it practically came with advance notice.
Hal easily countered, and soon they were grappling like amateur wrestlers. Smythe landed a few lucky punches; Hal countered by introducing Smythe’s nose to his armored forearm. Smythe tackled Hal again, pinning him against the car, twisting his arm painfully.
"Not again," Hal groaned, pulling a smoke bomb from his pocket and blindly tossing it into Smythe's face.
The agent staggered back, coughing theatrically. Hal quickly fired two bean bags from his launcher, both connecting satisfyingly with Smythe's face. As Smythe crumpled, Hal raised his right palm, pressed the button on the inside of his elbow, and delivered a now-properly-named Diana Ross directly to Smythe’s left buttock.
Smythe lay on the ground, whimpering, when Vicky’s voice crackled over Hal’s earpiece.  “Hal! Get to the car.  You need to see this!”
Hal stepped over Smythe, opened the car door, and sat down in the driver’s seat.  On the monitor, an ominous figure appeared, dressed in flowing red robes with an elaborate red headdress.  His face was hidden behind an expressionless white theater mask, and he stood in the middle of a grand room, the visible walls adorned with long, flowing blue velvet draperies entirely lit from below by a series of candles.  He stood motionless, elbows at his side, palms up as if in some sort of incantation.
“Good evening, my children.  I am the Hierophant, and I have come to lead all of you to the light.  For too long, you have cowered and suffered, but no more.  I promise you a new dawn, and a city of prosperity and happiness unlike any you’ve ever seen ... right after we burn this one to the ground.”
