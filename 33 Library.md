The sun was just setting as Hal pulled the Roadster into the Riverside neighborhood.  He was careful to park several blocks away on a mostly deserted street so as not to cause undue attention and waited the last few minutes before exiting under the cover of relative darkness. 
The Cavalier Street branch of the library was an unassuming one-story building located in the middle of a residential area.  He began by circling the block, studying the building from a distance.  It would do no good to wander through the front door dressed as he was, particularly if they had guessed wrong and this was not, in fact, the Hierophant’s target. 
As it was Sunday, the library had closed two hours earlier at 6 PM, and the staff had long since gone home.  Hal stood on the corner directly in front of the building, with its oversized windows providing a view of much of the front face.  He was rewarded with the faint flicker of lights coming from inside.  “I’m seeing flashlights,” he reported back to the man cave. 
“Could be a security guard?” Barry offered. 
“More than one, in a neighborhood branch? It’s not exactly a hotbed of crime.” 
“Okay, so now you just need to find a way in,” Vicky said.  “Aside from the front doors, there’s a fire exit on the south side and an employee entrance in the back.” 
Hal made his way around to the back of the building, sure enough, the door was unlocked. He slipped inside, finding himself in a break room barely big enough for a microwave and a questionable coffee maker. There was also a wine rack that looked more curated than the library’s new releases shelf.  There was no door, and from the hall he could hear hushed voices in the main room beyond. 
He peered down the hall, hearing hushed voices drifting from the main room. “Nice work, team. Now what?”
“So, what’s your plan, Sherlock?” Vicky asked. “Actually, what’s his plan?”
“There’s only one way to find out,” Hal said and strode into the library, not bothering to conceal himself or take cover.  He spotted four people busying themselves in various corners of the library.  None of them paid any attention to him. 
Barry’s voice crackled over the comm. “Any sign of the Hierophant?” 
“No sign. Just the backup dancers.” Hal hopped up onto the circulation desk for a better view. “Hey, folks, great to see you. Where’s your boss? I was hoping for a big, world-ending monologue.” 
At that moment, something hard and literary slammed into the back of Hal’s skull. He toppled off the desk, landing in a heap next to John Grisham’s “Sparring Partners.” One of the henchmen had beaned him with a hardcover. Obviously, a graduate of the WWE School of Literature and Bad Jokes. 
“Taking on a bit of light reading, Eidolon?” the Hierophant said, cackling maniacally over the library PA system. 
“Seriously?!  That’s your banter?!” Hal shouted back. “Come on man. You’ve got to leave these things to the experts.”  With that he picked up the book in one hand and charged his attacker, flinging the book like a Frisbee in front of him.  The henchman raised his hand to block the book, and Hal used the distraction to slam into him with a football tackle and send him headfirst into the shelves behind them. 
“Your commitment to such rugged violence. It’s spine-tingling,” was the response from the overhead speakers. 
Hal winced and looked around for the front desk where the Hierophant could have gotten access to the PA system. There was no sign of him. “Seriously, you’re really bad at this,” he shouted for good measure. 
“And, you’re not even trying. The joke pages of the last issue of Reader’s Digest had wittier banter than you,” the voice taunted.
“He’s right, Punkin’. Pull something out of your obscure reference desk, quick,” Vicky said over the comm. 
“No, no, they’re doing puns.  I’m more of a metaphor guy. I'm not good at puns.”
“You need to match him, Punkin', for the honor of the team,” Vicky said.
“What about ‘I’m going to dewy-decimate you?’” Barry offered. 
“Oh, that’s good, but a bit nerdy, don’t you think?  Maybe he should say something like, ‘I’m gonna have to fine you because your ass-kicking is overdue!’” 
“Yeah, that’s a good one.  I vote for that one, Hal.”
“Come on, guys, I can’t use any of those. You’re worse than he is.”  Hal had crawled between a row of shelves to get a view of the front desk and confirmed there was no sign of activity there.  The Hierophant must be using the wall phone near the fire exit, he decided, crouching low and starting to make his way in that general direction. 
There was a scream of voices, followed by a loud crash, then a second crash, and a third a few seconds later.  By the time Hal realized what was happening, the shelf next to him had already started tipping like a domino under the weight of the row next to it.  He threw himself to the ground and instantly found himself buried under a pile of physics books as shelves continued to tumble one after the other, all the way to the front of the building. 
“You’re right, of course,” The Hierophant said.  “I’m not very good at puns.  I can only blame my shelf.”  He cackled as he hung up the receiver.  Everyone shouted and started laughing as they ran for the back door, leaving him pinned in the reference section. 
“Are you okay?  What happened?” Vicky asked. 
“He dropped most of the 530s on me.” 
“The what?!” 
“He trashed the library,” Hal said. 
“That was his big plan?” Barry asked, confused.
Hal sighed. “It would seem so.” 
“That’s about the dumbest plan I’ve ever heard.  Who’s even going to notice something like that?” Vicky tsked.
Barry chimed in, “Where is he now?” 
“I suspect he’s getting away.” 
“Then get after him!” Vicky demanded. 
“I’m buried under the collective works of Richard Feynman here!  It’s heavy stuff.”  Hal struggled to move books in front of him over to the side, scooted forward a few inches, and then repeated the process. It was at least 20 feet to freedom, and at his current rate of progress, it would take at least 30 minutes of shifting and maneuvering to get there.  He was damned lucky he was able to dive for cover against the next row of shelves or he would have been completely crushed. Despite his protests of not being a fighter, this Hierophant definitely had a homicidal streak in him.  He would have to be more careful in the future. 
Eventually, Hal managed to crawl to the end of the row and out from under the pile of books.  He pulled himself to his feet and inspected the damage.  The henchmen were thorough.  They’d gone to nearly every section in the library and toppled the shelves.  Practically every book in the library had been knocked down in a domino-like cascade.   
“I suppose now’s the time to call Smythe,” Hal said, defeated.  “He’s going to want to check this out.” 
Barry and Vicky both groaned.
