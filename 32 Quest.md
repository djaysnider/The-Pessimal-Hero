It was 2 PM on Sunday when Hal finally stumbled out of bed, clutched a mug of coffee like it owed him money, and dragged himself down to the command center. Barry and Vicky were already hunched over glowing monitors like two caffeinated hackers trying to break nuclear codes. Hal felt like he’d been run over by a truck. Which was almost accurate, considering the previous night had included a high-speed chase, gunfire, assaulting a federal officer (by accident, mostly), and a dramatic showdown with his arch-nemesis, who casually launched a plan to destroy the city.  That definitely made this a two-cup morning.
Hal regarded his friends with a bleary eye, “Morning guys.” 
“Uh, Hal,” Barry said pointing to one of the screens which showed a local news broadcast.  “Please tell me you didn’t use your real name when you bought the Roadster.” 
“The ticker at the bottom of the screen read, ‘Vigilante arrested.  Eidolon revealed to be local crypto-currency millionaire Brad Copley.’” The footage showed a half-naked, bewildered guy in cuffs being dragged out of a mansion by agents in stereotypical black suits and dark glasses, while a woman in a designer bedsheet screamed like she’d just lost her front-row Coachella tickets.
“No, of course not.  I just gave him a wad of cash and had him sign over the title. I even kept his plates on it since I figured it would end up getting trashed before I could make it all legal.” 
Vicky arched her eyebrows, “Trashed?!  You are NOT trashing my car, Punkin'.” 
“Okay, fine, maybe it wasn’t the best organized plan, but you're smart, you’d have figured something out,” Hal said, holding up a hand. 
Barry sat down in his seat and pulled a recording of the Hierophant’s broadcast up. “Okay, so we’ve been going over the footage of the Hierophant from yesterday, along with your conversation with him last night.  We were hoping we could use that to predict his next move.  We know from your exchange last night that his grand plan starts today.” 
“Right,” Hal nodded.  “The question is, where is he going to strike first?” 
“Unfortunately, he’s mad as a thermos of fish, but he doesn’t tend to drone on about specifics the way good Bond villains do.  We think the closest he came was this line here.”  
Barry clicked the “play” icon on the video, and the Hierophant started speaking. “From their neglected troves of knowledge to their safe havens of recreation, my legions will spread out ...” 
“Now,” Barry continued, “if we took that as a literal time reference, then shouldn’t we be able to assume that this grand campaign is going to start at the ‘neglected troves of knowledge?’” 
“He’s planning something at the University,” Hal suggested. 
“We thought of that. This guy certainly has nothing but disdain for wealth, but he seems to respect intelligence and education,” Vicky said.
Hal stroked his chin, deep in thought. “So, he’d make a pretty lousy politician.”
“Either way, ‘neglected troves’ seems emotionally wrong and doesn’t seem to fit the profile.” 
Hal snapped a finger. “Public library, then.”
“Great, how many of those are there?” 
Hal shrugged. “Big, important statement?  The main library?” 
Barry grabbed a written transcript off the desk. “No, look at the rest of the speech.  He references both the sports stadium and the Riverside neighborhood.  What’s the closest library to those locations?” 
“Triangulating,” Vicky smiled, impressed, and pulled up the area on the map.  “Looks like the Cavalier Street branch.” 
“Are we still not going to alert Smythe or the authorities?” Barry asked. 
“Alert them to what?  So far, we’ve got a hunch and a villain who speaks in rejected slam poetry cliches. I’ll check it out. If I find something, then we can  go full Bat-Signal.” 
“Are you sure?  You do remember he’s loaded up with military-grade stun grenades, micro-drones, at least six henchmen, and a wardrobe budget straight out of The Purge: LARP Edition, right” 
Hal walked over to the mannequin and started removing the pieces of the Eidolon costume.  “All the more reason to scout it out and give them good intel before we call them in,” he said. 
“I don’t know,” Barry said helping Hal with the costume.  “I have a feeling this one is a bit bigger than the Eidolon.” 
“Barry, I can handle it. Besides, it’s obvious he’s suffering from textbook erotomania where I’m concerned.”
Barry winced, “Oh geez, man, this like Marcy Burton in the fourth grade. How many times do I have to tell you: just because someone says your name doesn’t mean they want to have sex with you!”
“Erotomania,” Hal rolled his eyes.  “He thinks I’m fixated with him.  By becoming the Hierophant, he thinks he’s answering my call to action.  You see it all over his language.  By dressing up and becoming my opposite image, he’s satisfying some schizophrenic delusion.”
“Oh, like John Hinckley being in love with Jodie Foster until he thought she rejected him and he went and shot the President,” Barry said.
Hal started to protest, “Yes, well--”
“Or Charles Manson murdering all those people because he thought the Beatles were telling him to in their songs?” Vicky offered?
“But on the other ha--”
Barry cut him off. “Oh, and remember that crazy prison inmate who thought Justin Bieber had broken up with her telepathically and hired a rogue janitor squad to castrate him at one of his concerts?”
Vicky sat up, obviously upset, “Now do I LOOK like the kind of person who follows Justin Bieber?  What the hell do you think I do in my spare time?  Write him love notes with little hearts for eyes and burn candles next to pictures of him in a little shrine I keep in the corner of my room? No way, that spot is reserved for Barry White!”
Hal threw up his hands in defeat, “Guys, you heard him.  He was convinced that we were on the same team and that I would understand his great plan.  I don’t think he wants to harm me.” 
“Yeah,” Barry confirmed, “and I also remember he was extremely disappointed when you shunned him and refused to join up with him.  What happens the next time you do that?  He’s obviously insane.  What is he capable of when you shatter the reality of his psychotic delusions?  He’s going to come at you with chicken scissors and remove your little eidolon.[^fn1] We really need to get a psychiatrist on staff before you go poking crazy people with sticks.” 
“I’ll be careful,” Hal said, slipping on the trench coat.  “Besides, the point of the mission isn’t to play it safe.” 
“I remember,” Barry said, defeated.

[^fn1]:  Obligatory Scott Sigler reference...
