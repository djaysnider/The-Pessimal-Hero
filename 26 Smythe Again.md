Hal regarded the business card for a second, not knowing what to make of his current situation.  Who the hell was Samuel Hamilton-Smythe? What the hell was the Department of Inner-city Logistics and Domestic Operations?  Most importantly, how the hell did he know to be here when even Hal didn’t until a couple of hours earlier?  He heard Vicky and Barry arguing off-mic in his ear.  
Shouts came from the alley behind him.  The scooter gangs, now on foot, had recovered themselves, ditched their normal modes of transportation, and were still pursuing him on foot.  “You’re probably going to want to get out of here,” Hal said and started around the car to the driver’s side door.
“Oh, right,” Smythe said calmly, “your enthusiastic fans.”  He tapped his left ear twice.  Hidden earpiece, Hal decided, and wished his own hidden backup was a bit more present.
Instantly, three hidden police cars screeched into view, lights blazing, blocking the alley and startling the gang members into submission. Officers poured out, guns drawn, shouting commands. Distant sirens announced even more backup.
In Hal's earpiece, Vicky huffed indignantly, “Don’t let this International Man of Mystery Meat intimidate you, Punkin’.  You haven’t done anything wrong, and he knows it. Otherwise, he'd be arresting you right now, too.”
“Well, it seems you have things well in hand, Agent Smythe,” Hal said in his best impersonation of Superman at the end of a Super Friends episode, “So, if you’ll excuse me, I need to be on my way.” Hal grabbed the handle of the door and found it locked.  He’d missed cool by just a smidge.
“Dammit, he’s got control of the Roadster,” Vicky growled in his ear. “Give me a minute.”
“If you could spare me just a few minutes of your time, I’d be very grateful.”
Hal shrugged and stepped back from the car but made sure to keep it between himself and Smythe.  “It seems you leave me no choice.”
“This little superhero act of yours is becoming inconvenient for my department,” Smythe began.
“This guy used to be FBI.  What the hell is a Department of Inner-city Logistics and Domestic Operations?” Vicky muttered.  Hal could hear she was still typing.
“What exactly is your ‘department?’  I’ve never heard of them.”
“Are you stalling so Ms. Montanna can track down my home phone?” Smythe arched a cool eyebrow.  “Or, is Mr. Chase gives you pointers on how to incapacitate me in three moves?  It doesn’t matter.  Since you asked, in addition to consulting with local law enforcement, my department is charged with curtailing the activities of people such as yourself.  People who undermine legitimate law enforcement and endanger themselves as well as innocent people.”
“Your concern for my well-being is touching, but there’s really no reason to worry about--”
“It’s not you I’m worried about.  Based on reports and interviews with witnesses, you’re obviously very well-trained, with extensive experience taking on superior numbers, and you’ve been well-equipped and well-financed by someone.  Still, we can’t have you interfering with legitimate police operations.”
“I'm hardly out to undermine law enforcement--”
“That may be so, but can you say the same for your cohorts? Do you know what Mr. Chase did during the war?  Don’t get me started on Ms. Montana’s history of trouble with the authorities. You, yourself, virtually check all of the boxes of a psychopath and future Netflix documentary subject.”
“Look, I’ve heard just about enough--”
“You have no idea how dangerous your teammates are. Or, maybe you do know, and that’s why you recruited them.  Either way, we’re going to have to ask you to stand your little operation down.”
“Oh, bring it, Badgey McPowertripp!  I’m not afraid of your skinny butt,” Hal heard Vicky say in his ear.
Barry chimed in, sounding less calm and collected than Vicky. “Don’t listen to him.  He’s just trying to get in our heads and make us suspicious of each other. There’s no law against vigilantism. He can’t do a damned thing to you.  You’re totally fine. Mostly.”
“Well,” Hal said, “you can ask. I don’t think you can legally do anything about it, however.”
“Vigilantism is not technically illegal, true,” Smythe said calmly.  “It’s all those little things you do while being a vigilante that add up to real trouble -- trespassing, breaking and entering, assault and battery, intentionally setting off a fire alarm--”
“Hey, the fire alarm was accidental,” Hal pointed out.
“-- and that’s just what you’re directly on the hook for.  Your associates are in even more trouble.  We could easily haul Ms. Montanna in for any number of violations of the CFAA, SCA, ECPA, and NACA, and nobody really wants your financier to get a visit from the IRS.”
He heard Vicky laughing in his ear, “Awww, Punkin’, he doesn’t even know who you are.  He thinks you’re just hired muscle. He's grasping at straws, trying to frighten you off.”
“I don’t think you really understand what we’re doing,” Hal offered.
“You’re not a determinist, are you?” Smythe asked.
“A what?”
“Determinist. Like, is this somehow your destiny? Everything in your life has brought you to this point?”
Hal thought for a moment. “Well, no, not exactly.”
“So, you believe in free will, then.  Your moral code is in service to a higher power to get divine brownie points?”
"Neither," Hal admitted. "Just, you know, doing the right thing."
"Those people," Smythe groaned dramatically. "You're the worst. Give me religious zealots any day. I know their limits. But you 'just do the right thing' types inevitably mess everything up."
Hal tried following this logic and felt a mild headache approaching. "Uh, are you done? Because you're giving off supervillain monologue vibes."
Smythe shook his head. "The point is, vigilantes upset the system. Eventually, our 'right things' collide, and things get messy. Usually, I'd offer a convenient retirement fund to get you to walk away, but idealists like you? Impossible."
"Well, sorry for having inconvenient morals," Hal said sarcastically.
"My point exactly," Smythe said, exasperated. "Stop thinking. Just let the professionals handle justice."
Hal folded his arms across his chest. “Listen, you really don’t understand what’s going on here.”
“Actually, it’s apparent you still don’t understand.” Smythe tapped his earpiece twice, and Hal heard the Roadster doors unlock.  The driver’s side door popped open. “This was a friendly warning.  The ‘right thing’ to do now is to retire.  If we cross paths again, I’ll be forced to do my job and end this for you.”  With that, Smythe turned and walked towards the flashing lights of the police cars, where a half-dozen members of the scooter gangs were draped over the hoods in handcuffs.
Hal skulked into the car and quietly drove off.
