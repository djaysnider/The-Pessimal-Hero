“So, obviously, he knew about all of us, but seemed to know the most about you.  Anything you think you need to be telling us?”  Hal had slipped out of the Eidolon garb and into a pair of sweatpants.  He popped open a beer, straddled a barstool, and stared directly at Vicky.
Vicky shook her head. “Look, Punkin’, if I thought I was going to get either of you into trouble, I would never have--” 
“Just tell us who he is,” Hal was calm, but out of patience.
Vicky stood up and started pacing.  She looked at the screens as if the words might be written there, then looked at Barry and Hal, what Hal took to be almost the look of a guilty mother.  “Look, after my boy was killed, I did a little detour into villainville. Revenge became my therapy---I hacked everyone responsible: gangsters, cops, even the DA. Emails, identities, embarrassing browser histories---nothing was off-limits. Their credit scores tanked faster than crypto. Some got fired. Some were arrested. Turns out revenge isn't the soul-cleanser advertised. I ruined all their lives.”  She sighed, tears welling up in her eyes.  “It didn’t make my pain go away though.  It didn’t bring my boy back.  In fact, I probably felt worse after I had won.  I decided that meant the world still owed me something. That’s where I went a bit too far and got caught.”
Hal was slack-jawed.  Barry was the first to speak.  “What the hell did you do?”
“Oh, Muffin, that’s going in my book.  Needless to say, it put me on the radar of one Special Agent Samuel Hamiton-Smythe of the FBI’s National Cyber Investigative Whatever Task Force. He had me dead to rights.  I was looking at wire fraud, computer fraud, identity theft, computer trespassing, wire interception, a whole list of things.  It could have gone very badly for me, but Sammy understood what I was going through.  He helped me redeem myself and satisfied my thirst for justice.”  She chuckled and wiped her eyes.  “Anyway, after that, I had to go on the down-low -- new town, new job, new name.  The works.”  
Hal finally got his mouth working again. “You’re in the Federal witness relocation program?”
“Well, I was, until I broke the one big rule about not making their job difficult.”
“So, who the hell is the Department of Inner-city Logistics and Domestic Operations?” Barry asked.
“Some new FBI division, maybe?  I don’t know.  The point is, he’s right, you boys haven’t done anything wrong.  He may be able to get me for this stuff, but he’s not going to hang you out as accomplices.  That’s just an idle threat.  He doesn’t work like that.”
“So, what are we going to do?” Barry asked.
“Obviously, the mission isn’t over,” Hal said. “You guys don’t have to go on, but I do.  Maybe it’s best that I go it alone from here on in.”
“Oh, come on, Hal. It's not just about your tragic backstory anymore. If we bail, License to Mansplain will just track us down next time you play Eidolon. Hospice care isn't glamorous, sure, but federal prison has even worse room service.”
“I’m not afraid to die. You think I’m afraid of prison? That’s not what this is about?”
“Yeah, that’s another thing he was right about too, you know.”
“What’s that?”
“You’re only concerned with what you think is right, and that makes you more dangerous than somebody who has a moral compass.”
“What are you saying?”
“I’m saying, walking into dangerous situations without regard for yourself or non-combatant casualties, goading people into fighting you instead of diffusing a potentially violent situation, almost killing people by dropping an entire second-story loft on them?  That’s the Punisher, man. Not you.”
“Barry, we trained for this.  We talked about this--”
“We were talking about taking a bullet meant for somebody else.  Not starring in a low-budget action flick,” Barry said, raising his voice for the first time.
“So, what are you saying?  Are you out now?” Hal looked at Barry accusingly and saw the big man shy away from him for just a second.  They had been friends for decades, but this was the first real fight they’d ever had.  Hell, Hal thought, this was the first time he’d ever stood his ground with Barry about anything.  Something in Barry’s eyes said he was thinking the same thing.  He stood up straight and walked over to Hal.
“Hal.” Barry grabbed him by the arms.  “You’re my best friend.  I told you I’d be here and that I would give you whatever you need.  I’m not going to go back on that.  I just want you to think about what we signed up for and what you’re asking of us.  It’s hard enough helping my best friend die. It’s even harder to watch him die inside and become somebody else.  Remember when you thought this up?  You said you wanted your death to have meaning and your life to have purpose.  Is this really what you were talking about?”
Hal frowned, took a long swing of beer, and nodded.  He glanced over at Vicky.  “What do you think then?”
Vicky sat down and sighed loudly.  “I’m not as worried about your moral compass as Muffin here, but I do think I got you into this, and I’m probably the only chance you have of pulling it off.  Frankly, I don’t care what happens to me as long as you see this through.”
Hal nodded.  “Thank you both. I get the message. Let’s get off the radar and go back to the original plan -- no more taking on the scooter gangs single-handedly, no more spy missions to enemy headquarters, no more trying to get ourselves on the 11 o'clock news.  Tomorrow night we are born again.  We go back to the shadows and back to basics.”
