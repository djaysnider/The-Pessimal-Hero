That does sting a bit.”

Four dozen motorized scooters buzzed past them, their riders whooping and yelling, fists pumping in the air.  Each rider stopped behind a white cargo truck where boxes covered in Chinese writing were being unloaded and picked up one of the weapons being offered there.  Some chose Molotov cocktails, others picked up white painted bricks, and the less pyrotechnically inclined chose aluminum softball bats.

You’re an intelligent person, your manners are impeccable, and you’ve obviously got considerable physical prowess with the restraint to use it only as a last resort when reason fails.  Honestly, I was hoping there might be a place for you in whatever brave new world arises out of the ashes of tonight.”  With that, he stuck his fingers under his mask and blew a long, shrill whistle while making a circular motion in the air with his free hand. Oh, Eidolon, I’m really disappointed you think so little of me.”  He let out an exasperated growl. “Oh, come on, I guess you’ll be surprised then.”  He motioned for the goons holding Hal to follow him and proceeded towards the park exit behind the riders.
By the time they reached the edge of the commercial district, the first block and a half were in complete ruin.  Nearly all of the buildings had their windows and doors smashed in, with about half of them currently in flames.  The entire night sky was lit up in bright orange, and smoke was beginning to sting at Hal’s eyes.  He heard the first sirens off in the distance.  Police, fire, and ambulance were on their way.  Even without Vicky’s precise monitoring of their movements, he guessed no more than two minutes, and this would all hopefully be under control.
Or would it?
Four blocks away at the far end of the strip, Hal saw the henchmen had pushed two delivery vans together so that they blocked the street and then set fire to them.  The resulting inferno would serve as a barricade for the next several minutes, keeping police and fire personnel out of the western half of the neighborhood.  Meanwhile, the equipment truck that armed the henchmen had pulled up behind them, blocking the eastern half.  The driver was already working to get it lit on fire as well.  Within a few minutes, the scooter gangs would effectively have complete control of three blocks of the Riverside neighborhood.
“You see it now,” The Hierophant said.
“I don’t see two years of planning.  I see chaos and mayhem and a lot of innocent people getting hurt.”
The Hierophant shook a finger at Hal.  “But do you?  No!  My men have very strict instructions that nobody is to be harmed.  The businesses are the targets. Life is sacred. There is to be no show of force against civilians except in self-defense!”
“Did you make sure the police got that memo.  As soon as they put those fires out, they’re going to come storming in here in full riot gear and put down this little insurrection with indiscriminate violence.”
“You’re starting to see it, but you’re leaving out the entire point of this operation.  The part I spent two years judging, estimating, and planning.”
“What?!  What am I missing?!”
“The bit that happens in those fifteen minutes,” The Hierophant said as if it should have been obvious to everyone.
Then Hal saw it.  It began with one restaurant owner, by all appearances a kindly man of Asian descent, seeing his livelihood going up in flames while those who had sworn to serve and protect him were trapped two blocks away.  The proprietor did the only thing he could.  Grabbing a pan and a kitchen knife, he charged the gang member who stood laughing in front of what used to be his front door and beat the ever-loving heck out of him using what Hal could only assume was a combination of advanced wok cooking techniques, movie Kung-fu, and long-forgotten military training in knife fighting.  It wasn’t graceful, it was barely effective, but it succeeded in one thing: inspiring the woman who worked at the nail salon next door.  
The salon owner came running out of her establishment, brandishing a pistol, which she fired into the air several times, causing ruffians to board their scooters and start up the street.  The salon owner was followed by the gift shop worker next to her, then the tobacco store owner came out brandishing a shotgun.  Next, some students who lived in the apartments above the shops came down, golf clubs in hand, and began taking on the riders.  Within ten minutes, the neighborhood had gone full Les Misérables, only with scooters and softball bats instead of bayonets.
“Your Maccabees are going to lose this war handily, Hierophant.”
“As was always the intent.  Do you still not see the genius of my design?”
“Getting your ass kicked standing in the street dressed like that? While both your henchmen and innocent citizens get injured or killed fighting for you?! It’s not genius!  It’s insane!”
The Hierophant was laughing hysterically.  “The people will fight!  The temple will rise! It’s exactly as I predicted!” 
“Only as long as those barricades are burning,” Hal said, eyeing the two trucks at the edge of the neighborhood.  He saw myriad flashing lights and people coming from behind them, struggling to get into a position from which they could fight the oil and gas fires. No doubt, the police had arrived first and were blocking the fire trucks and ambulances, creating a nightmare of logistics to rearrange before anything could be done.
He had to try, Hal decided, and without thinking, clocked the henchman standing on his right with an armored forearm, shattering his nose.  Then, retrieving the taser from his belt holster, he fired directly into the chest of the man on his left.  Panicked, the Hierophant ran into the battling crowd, shouting orders at his henchmen as he went.  Within seconds, Hal had lost him in the smoke and confusion.
Hal grabbed a softball bat and started running westward towards the two burning trucks.  He arrived to find two dozen police and fire officers staring intently at the flames, oblivious to the carnage that lay beyond, content to let the fires just burn themselves out, settling for containment rather than extinguishing. They were a bag of marshmallows away from being relaxed about the entire situation.
“Officers!” Hal shouted, waving his hands to get their attention.
“It’s that Idolguy!” One of the officers said and moved to where he could see Hal more clearly.  “What the hell are you doing in there!?”

The hell there isn’t, Hal decided, assessing the sizes of the trucks and the relative positions of the officers surrounding them.  He started back up the street and spotted exactly what he was looking for, a large diesel delivery truck dropping off produce to the Indian restaurant.  The driver had found himself trapped in the neighborhood, and having no real skin in the game, decided to hide inside his truck and wait out the festivities.
Hal jumped up on the side of the truck and opened the driver's side door.
“What the hell are you supposed to be?” the driver asked, regarding Hal’s costume.

“Exactly,” Hal smiled, though it was not visible through the gaiter.  You’ll probably want to get out now.

As he closed the distance, he did a series of mental calculations in his head, trying to picture the vehicles as pool balls to be pocketed instead of flaming death traps he could easily crush people with.  At the last minute, he swerved and, rather than splitting them down the middle, he hit the front quarter panel of the truck on the right at just the right angle that the truck spun ninety degrees and slid onto the sidewalk from the street.
Hal ground the gears for a moment, finally getting the truck into reverse and backing it up to the sidewalk on the opposite side of the street, embarrassingly failing to notice a lovely red Camaro that had previously parked in the location he chose to come to a stop.  Glancing at his handiwork, he realized that he’d made a two-carwidth gap in the flames.  Police and firemen were already scrambling to their cars and preparing to head for the burning businesses at the other end of the neighborhood.  Without apologizing to the owner, an oversight he would later realize was extremely rude, he shut the engine off, tossed the keys to his passenger, and ran as fast as he could back to where the action was.
By the time he reached the east end of the neighborhood, things were far worse than where he had left them.  Blood had been spilled, and now local residents were showing no mercy to the scooter gangs.  Bits of twisted scooter were everywhere.  Bodies lay all over the street.  The police, too, weren’t exactly being subtle about crowd dispersal.  Several canisters of tear gas had been jettisoned, and now they were marching in formation with shields and billy clubs, hitting anyone who dared come close to them, civilian and gang member alike.
It was completely out of control.
Being, for the most part, behind the police, Hal surveyed the scene.  At the very least, this may be his last chance to die the heroic death that he was hoping for, and with the current rush of adrenaline he was feeling, this would probably be the only time he could bring himself to actually do it.  
It had been a good life, he decided.  He’d had some good friends; he’d had some good times. In the end, everyone parted on good, if final, terms.  Barry had been right, though.  Thinking he could reform or bring in The Hierophant was a fool’s errand.  It wasn’t the mission.  It was a job best left to professionals.  While the riots and ensuing carnage were, by admission, part of the Hierophant's grand plan and design, Hal was witnessing firsthand how quickly things could get out of control.  The best plan was his first plan: Just a quiet, anonymous death saving someone who was deserving.  It was the right thing to do.  No moral ambiguity about it.  Barry had been right about everything.  Except the bit about Hal not being able to pick his own death.
He laughed as he started humming Rachmaninov’s Second Concerto, second movement to himself and ran head-first into the crowd, pulling gang members off civilians and disarming them, and urging residents to get indoors and let the police handle the situation.
In the confusion, he caught sight of two ash-covered figures fighting.  Grabbing a discarded softball bat off the ground, he ran towards the scuffle and separated the two combatants, screaming at the one who had been lying on the ground on the losing end of the battle.  “Go, get inside, the Police can handle it from here.”
To his amazement, it wasn’t a local resident. He found himself staring directly down into the masked face of The Hierophant, his head bloodied and his cloak torn.  He turned and saw that the assailant was the Asian restaurant owner, now brandishing a softball bat much like the one Hal was carrying.  Rather than provoke the situation further, Hal laid his bat on the ground and held his hands in front of him, gesturing for the restaurateur to stand down.
The man, who had seen his business go up in flames moments before, and who very nearly had avenged himself on the man responsible, was having none of it, however.  With a cry of rage and determination, he hefted the bat and swung it with all his might at Hal’s head.
