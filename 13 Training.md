It was an absurdly grueling and seemingly eternal six weeks. In fact, Hal became quite convinced at several points that death was imminent---or at least significantly ahead of schedule. Barry, true to his promise and perhaps secretly enjoying Hal's misery, relentlessly drilled Hal in both the physical and mental skills essential for surviving one-against-many street battles with non-lethal weapons. Still, Hal couldn't shake the feeling that Barry’s heart wasn’t in it.  If enthusiasm were a sport, he’d still be on the bench, demonstrated mainly by Barry’s frequent sighs, eye-rolls, and occasional muttered prayers for patience.
Without the burden of a day job, Hal was able to quickly grasp the fundamentals of kung fu, karate, judo, tai chi, and Manipuri -- the last few via instructional YouTube videos just to give him something to surprise Barry with during their sparring matches, which they most certainly did. It turned out that tai chi, a discipline primarily involving very slow movements and deep breaths, offered no advantage in a high-speed sparring match. Even more embarrassingly, Manipuri proved to be a traditional dance form from Northeast India and not, in fact, a martial art---a discovery Hal made mid-match while gracefully tandavaing into a right hook.  On the bright side, he’d also slimmed down two sizes, put on approximately fifteen pounds of lean muscle, and went from groaning when he carried a basket of laundry up the stairs to benching nearly 180 pounds.
On days when Barry worked, Hal spent his time working out while listening to audio books on urban warfare, battle tactics, military strategy, and criminal psychology.  At night, the two would practice sparring, wrestling, or quizzing each other with various scenarios that the Eidolon might face in a typical night on patrol.  In all, even if he got lucky and managed to find his perfect fate on the first night, and all of this training and accumulation of knowledge was for nothing, Hal decided he still had the time of his life and wouldn’t change a thing.  On the other hand, if he wasn’t lucky, then he was about to become a force to be reckoned with on the mean streets of the city.
Vicky dropped by regularly, usually on weekends or when a particularly sensitive delivery arrived at the anonymous PO box and needed to be immediately set up.  Her primary task was to convert the other half of the man cave into the Eidolon’s command center, where she and Barry could observe, inform, and otherwise provide support for Hal while he was deployed in the field.  With the practically unlimited budget Hal had given her, she’d managed to construct a monstrosity of computers, displays, receivers, relays, radios, and a particularly high-end sound system which, when she wasn’t monitoring weather conditions, police scanners, and traffic patterns, served as her own personal stereo through which she attempted to convert Hal and Barry to what she referred to as the “Golden Pipes” of Chuck Brown.
One skill which the guys had not expected Vicky to possess, though were pleased to learn she did, was her talent as a seamstress, which proved instrumental on the last week of Hal’s training when the final components of the all-important “superhero suit” arrived from a black-market arms dealer in Uzka. Hal wasn’t sure where Uzka was, but the online catalog Vicky showed him was amazingly thorough and contained everything from arms and ammunition to surplus kidneys and physicians to transplant them.
 “Last box,” Vicky said as she kneed open the door to the man cave. The three had long ago passed the formalities of knocking, saying hello, or even looking up to see who had just come barging into what was supposed to be a super-secret superhero command center.  The three had formed a tightly-oiled machine.
“Holy crap, today is the day!” Hal said excitedly.  “This is really happening!”  He got up from the couch and walked over to the mannequin in the corner of the room.  Many very cool and influential people owned mannequins, he thought, but none were cool enough to own two mannequins, both made from plaster casts of their own body. Honestly, lying naked in a tub of plaster turned out to be the weirdest part of that experience. Having the cute 19-year-old art student spend two hours slathering him in Vaseline and later delicately separating him from the dried plaster without ripping out all his body hair was the second weirdest.
Hal went over and admired the mannequins, which were dressed in the rudimentary beginnings of a superhero suit, one with the outer components, the other with the under armor.  He had modeled the aesthetic on a combination of his three favorite heroes.  First, the outfit consisted of an ornate utility belt containing several pouches and pockets, with additional pockets in the chest, thighs, and upper arms.  These contained everything he could think of as well as a few goodies he’d borrowed from the Batman technical manual including caltrops, smoke bombs, stink bombs, flash bang grenades, tear gas, pepper spray, stun gun, tazer, three protein bars, and his cell phone.
In lieu of a cape, which would not only look conspicuous but also potentially hinder movement, he instead adopted the long trench coat often favored by The Shadow in a lightweight gray fabric that concealed the weaponry, armor, and gadgets he wore, protected him from the elements, and did not weigh him down with unnecessary bulk.  To allow maximum mobility in a fight, the coat had no actual sleeves, though it perfectly matched the cloth coverings on his armor to the point they appeared to be the same piece of clothing.  The coat also featured a wide hood that hid his features without restricting his peripheral vision.  This was his nod to Luke Skywalker in full Jedi mode entering Jabba’s palace at the beginning of Episode Six.
Also in line with the Jedi motif was the undersuit, which fit tunic-fashion, like a Jedi’s, for maximum movement and concealment of equipment.  The suit, very cleverly, had additional pouches and compartments in the arms and leggings into which the specially form-fit armor plates that had just arrived were slipped.  The plates were small, lightweight, and made of Kevlar, graphene, or carbon fiber and, except for the exposed area between, could easily stop a knife or low-caliber bullet and, with luck, provide a minimum of dispersal from the impact of a club or chain, should the need arise.
Barry handed Hal the individual pieces of armor, calling out the part numbers etched on each one and giving him a general idea on which part of the suit they would slip into -- LA4: left arm.  RT3: right thigh, and so forth. “I know I’ve asked you this a million times,” he said, “but are you sure you don’t want armor covering your chest or your head?  I mean one light tap with a lead pipe in either place, and you’re pretty much down and done.”
Hal shook his head. “And I’ve told you a million times, the armor is designed to keep the arms and legs functional and provide some protection from clubs and bludgeoning objects.  I’m hoping if I get shot or stabbed, then it will be quick, fatal, and reasonably painless.  I just don’t want to get shot in the leg and have to retire as a crime fighter before I am properly killed.”
Vicky sat down on the couch. “You know, I’ve listened to this conversation a million times, and it’s still the weirdest damned excuse I’ve ever heard.”  She rifled through the box and extracted an oddly shaped hunk of Kevlar.  “I almost forgot,” she said handing it to Hal, “This is my gift to you, Punkin’: TP4.5.”
TP? That didn't fit their numering scheme. Hal took the piece, turned it over in his hand, and tried to figure out what it was for.  Finally, he looked at her, defeated.
“It’s an iron shield for ‘Krull the Warrior King,’ Punkin'.”
Hal looked at Barry, saw no recognition there either, and raised an eyebrow to Vicky again.
“A steel bowl for the danger noodle?”
“A who for the what?”
“Dammit, Punkin’, don’t you make me say it!”
Hal continued to stare at her.
“A ‘Kevlar cage for your groin ferret!’  You may not care about a big guy breaking multiple ribs, but what if he hits further south? Or worse, what if somebody peels one of your berries with a knife? You're gonna go down with splat like a sack of herring and lose your enthusiasm for justice real quick, Punkin'.”
Hal let out a nervous laugh. “I actually hadn’t considered that.  It really doesn’t come up in comic books.  I’m pleased you were thinking of my ... er ...”
“Yeah, I was thinkin’ about it,” Vicky said dismissively. “Go ahead and ask me where I came up with the name for the part number.”
“I don’t think I will,” Hal said, defeated, as he slid the codpiece into place on the suit.
As final touches, Vicky had mounted a few pieces of distinctive nonlethal weaponry into the suit itself, fashioned a long-lasting battery pack and Bluetooth into the lower back, and topped it all off with a small, concealed police-issue body cam in the left nipple.  Next, she handed him two earpieces that could either be worn in the ear or stored inside the hood, where they connected to small, embedded speakers.  This allowed the “support team” to monitor and communicate with Hal in real time when he was in the field.  If nothing else, he wouldn’t feel alone out there.
“Why did you go with gray instead of black?” Barry asked, inspecting the suit inventory and connections.  “Black would give you much better concealment at night, especially with non-reflective fabrics like these.”
“To the ancient Greeks, gray was associated with wisdom, knowledge, and balance. Hence, it is the true color of justice. Plus, black is overdone by superheroes these days.  Batman, Spider-Man, the movie X-Men, and even Superman, after he died, all got black suits. Plus, we can always add a secondary color. Gray goes with everything.”
“Wow,” Vicky said without enthusiasm. “You’ve really thought this through,”
“Hey, what can I say, I’m a man of style.”
“No, you’re not,” Vicky corrected him.
“No, I’m not,” Hal admitted. 
Barry stepped back and regarded the finished product. “You do look good, though. Sort of like  The Question, with a hint of Green Hornet.”
“More like Moon Knight, if Moon Knight worked part-time at a Starbucks and wasn't allowed within 20 yards of a school,” Vicky said, rolling her eyes.
Both Barry and Hal looked at her.  “Hey, I knew I had to do my research to keep up with 'Mister Chase Me' and ‘Captain Loyalty the Walking Shampoo Commercial’ here, so I got a Disney+ subscription.”  
Barry smiled. “When are we going to take it for a trial run?”
Hal looked at both of them as if expecting a suggestion, then realizing it was his decision, sighed, took a deep breath, and said, “I suppose tonight.”
Barry appeared crestfallen.  Without even looking at Hal, he turned and walked over to the couch and sat down.
Hal looked at him, confused. “What’s wrong, Barr?”
“Nothing.  I ... I ... well, I mean I knew this day was coming.  It’s what we’ve been working toward all this time.  I just didn’t know how I’d feel when it finally arrived.”
“You don’t think I’m ready?”
“No, man.  You’re ready.  That’s the problem.  You’re ready to go out there and look for your perfect death.”  He shook his head, then turned to look at Hal.  “I mean, we did all that training, all this work, so you could go out there and do as much good as you could for as long as possible, but still in the back of your mind, if you see a chance to get that perfect, heroic sacrifice you’re looking for tonight, you’re gonna take it, aren't you?  So, that will make this the day I lose my best friend.”
Hal started pacing, suddenly feeling ashamed. “I’m sorry, man, I hadn’t thought of that. I didn’t think how hard this was going to be on you.  Now, every night I go out there, you’re gonna be feeling this way. I’ve had almost two months of thinking about my impending death nonstop.  I’ve accepted it.  I’ve planned for it.  I’ve pictured it in my head a million times.  I’m ready.”  He put a hand on the suit, making it feel real for a moment.  “You’re not, though. You did all this with me.  You stuck by me all this time because it was keeping your mind off of it.”
“It’s fine, Hal.  Don’t change your plan or give up your dream because I wasn’t ready.  You want this, and I told you I’d be here for you.  My feelings won’t change anything that’s going to happen, and I’m not letting you go out there alone.  Your support team is going to be here giving you 100% at all times.”
“You sure, man?”
Barry nodded.  “Let’s never have this conversation again.  We’ll shortcut it.  Every time you go out, instead of good luck, I’ll just say, ‘Ookla, ride!’  That’ll be our shortcut for that conversation we almost had.”  With that, he stood up, walked over to Hal, and gave him a hearty slap on the shoulder.
Vicky rolled her eyes and began turning on the array of equipment in front of her.  “You boys are amazingly dysfunctional.”
