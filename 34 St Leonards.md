“Seriously, trashing a library doesn’t sound like much of a plan if you want to evoke terror in the streets and incite people to change,” Vicky said.
Barry sipped at his coffee. “Maybe we’re looking at this all wrong. He's obviously a well-read person who values intelligence in others. That’s probably why he sees Hal as a kindred spirit who will join him on their Greek mythology pun war or whatever it is they’re on.”
“Oh, so we think he’s a bitter ex-librarian now?” Vicky muttered, pulling up old employee records. “Let’s see who got let go for reorganizing the romance section by emotional damage.”
Barry snapped his finger. “Their AV expert?”
Vicky nodded, “Could be. We’re definitely looking at someone with a personal grudge and a thesaurus.”
Hal was still asleep, which was unusual for him. However, the events of the last several days and the full-body smackdown courtesy of the physics section yesterday had earned him a bit of unconsciousness.  Neither Barry nor Vicky could bear to wake him up.
“Still, why is everybody targeting libraries all of a sudden?” Barry asked, then caught himself. They both looked at each other in amazement.  “How did we miss that?”
Vicky pulled up the notes from the garage bust.  “Scooter gangs were told to target a library, a church, and a park.  We found the library, so we should assume the church and the park are nearby.  So, this Hierophant guy is with Columbanus Enterprises?”
“There is no Columbanus Enterprises,” Vicky said.  “Every one of those addresses on those mysterious overseas shipments is for an abandoned building.”
“When do the packages arrive?”
“Sometime today.”
“So, why don’t we stake a couple out and try to get ahead in this game for once?”
“I thought of that,” Vicky said, nodding.  “However, you can bet our friend Sammy is going to have his people doing exactly the same thing.  Plus, it’s daylight, which makes it harder for us and easier for anyone watching for us.  No, we need to be working on figuring out which church they’re going to target and get ahead of them.”  She pulled up a map of the Riverside Neighborhood.
“St. Leonard’s.”
Vicky zoomed the map in further, “Hey, there IS a St. Leonard’s about a half a mile from the library.”
“It’s Hal’s family church, and it’s on the news,” Barry said, pointing to the television feed on one of the monitors. “We’re too late.”
The headline on the ticker read, “Local Irish Festival Attached by Scooter Gangs,” and the display was one of complete carnage as ruffians on scooters were seen turning over booths, smashing car windshields, and terrorizing the patrons, mostly older residents of the area.
“Better wake Punkin’,” Vicky said, not taking her eyes off the screen.
“I think you’re right,” Barry said and ran for the stairs.  “Hal!  Evil doesn’t sleep!”
